# Second-pass editorial audit reconciliation

Date: 2026-09-12  
Status: report only; proposed corrections are not approved or published

This reconciliation compares the independent Kimi and Claude returns with the current immutable `om-studies-2026-09-11-v7` article release, the pinned BSB TXT, the pinned Nestle 1904 morphology, the active word metadata, and the published comparison records. It does not treat either auditor's proposed wording as an instruction or as approval to alter the release.

## Result

Most of the substantive findings are real. The main defect class is not ordinary copyediting: phrase-sized quotations were replaced with whole verses or verse fragments, leaving grammar, references, and Greek-to-English gloss relationships broken. The current v7 bytes contain every Claude article excerpt and all but one Kimi excerpt exactly; Kimi's `amen` excerpt differs only because the audit used a curly apostrophe where v7 has an ASCII apostrophe. The underlying `amen` wording is present.

Recommended disposition:

- Accept the substance of 62 of Claude's 66 findings. Do not adopt the auditor's wording blindly; reconstruct each correction from the pinned BSB and the surrounding argument.
- Accept the substance of 22 of Kimi's 23 findings. Several duplicate Claude findings; for `proselytos`, accept the inconsistency but use the macron-bearing form rather than Kimi's proposed unmacronized normalization.
- Do not make the five purely conventional changes described under **No change recommended**.
- Add one material correction not promoted to a formal finding by either auditor: `leitourgia` must not derive “the work of the people” as the controlling literal meaning or build the theology of corporate worship from that derivation.

The accepted M4 scope remains accepted. These findings concern a successor to the immutable v7 Ordinary Means article release and one narrowly corrected comparison record; they do not authorize editing v7 in place.

- Colossians 1:15 ends with “the firstborn over all creation”; verse 16 begins “For in Him all things were created.”
