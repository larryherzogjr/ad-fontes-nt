### A. Public title

**Luke 22:43–44: Why Your Bible Has a Footnote Here**

### B. Public prose — source observation

At Luke 22:43–44 an angel strengthens Jesus in Gethsemane and his sweat becomes like drops of blood. Unlike most passages in this collection, **every edition compared here prints both verses** — the Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible, the Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Nestle's 1904 Greek edition, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus [C1]. Set them side by side and you will find no disagreement.

What you will find is a footnote. Both Berean editions carry the same one: that some manuscripts do not include these verses [C2]. That is a statement about handwritten copies, not about the printed editions on your screen, and it is the publishers' statement rather than one this note verifies or endorses.

The distinction matters. A comparison of editions can show you what publishers decided to print. It cannot show you what stands in manuscripts none of these editions reproduces.

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

So the honest answer to "why is there a footnote here?" is that the publishers are telling you something their own printed text does not show: that the manuscript evidence for these two verses is, on their account, divided. They print the verses and flag the question. That is a reasonable thing for an edition to do, and noticing it is a useful piece of literacy.

What this note cannot do is tell you how that question comes out. Assessing which manuscripts contain the passage, and how old they are, requires a critical apparatus and the training to read it. Neither is in view here, and a note that summarised the footnote as though it were a finding would be passing off a publisher's claim as its own.

What can be said is narrower and still worth saying. The Gethsemane account does not stand or fall on these two verses: Luke's narrative of the prayer, the cup, and the arrest surrounds them and is not in question anywhere. And a footnote in your Bible is not a warning label. It is an editor declining to keep something from you.
