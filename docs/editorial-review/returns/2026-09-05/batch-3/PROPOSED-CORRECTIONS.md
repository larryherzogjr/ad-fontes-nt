# Batch 3 — proposed factual corrections

Pending Larry Herzog Jr.’s decision. Originals remain unchanged.

Candidate 16: BLB ends with “withered,” not “the paralyzed.” The healing is reported in all editions but its wording differs (including Greek forms at John 5:8–9). The returned C3 explicitly says Nestle’s marker convention was not established.

Candidate 19: both parts of the MSB note concern textual differences; the returned “second” is ambiguous because the prose reverses the source note’s order.

Candidate 24: Nestle 1904 Romans 16:27 has τῶν αἰώνων in addition to the wording in RP2018/Boyd. The discussion of the weak and strong continues in Romans 15:1–7. Presence, placement and wording must remain distinct.

## candidate-16

**Returned:** The Berean Standard Bible and the Berean Literal Bible print none of it, ending verse 3 at "the paralyzed" and going straight to verse 5 [S1, S2].

**Proposed:** The Berean Standard Bible and the Berean Literal Bible print none of it, ending verse 3 with the list of the sick and going straight to verse 5 [S1, S2].

**Returned:** That is a third option — neither adopting nor removing, but flagging.

**Proposed:** The material is present and marked; the markers’ precise editorial meaning is not established by these chapter extracts.

**Returned:** and that account is identical across all seven editions.

**Proposed:** and all seven editions report that healing, though their wording is not identical.

## candidate-19

**Returned:** Two different kinds of difference in one note; only the second concerns whether a whole sentence stood in Luke's account.

**Proposed:** The wording difference at verse 33 and the presence of verse 34 are separate textual matters; the latter concerns whether a whole sentence stood in Luke's account.

## candidate-24

**Returned:** Nothing is added and nothing is dropped; the same three verses stand in a different position.

**Proposed:** The doxology is present in all seven editions, but its position differs. There are also smaller wording differences; Nestle 1904 has an additional “of the ages” at the end of verse 27 compared with Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd.

**Returned:** Because the words are the same, nothing in the doxology's content is in question. What placement changes is what the doxology closes. At the end of chapter 16 it seals the whole letter, following the greetings and the final benediction. After 14:23 it falls at the end of Paul's argument about the weak and the strong, and the letter then continues for two more chapters.

**Proposed:** This note concerns the doxology’s placement; smaller wording differences are a separate question. At the end of chapter 16 it closes the letter after the greetings. After 14:23 it comes before the continuation of Paul’s discussion of the weak and the strong in chapter 15, and the letter continues for two more chapters.

**Returned:** that its wording is not in dispute,

**Proposed:** that it is present in every edition compared here,

**Returned:** as though something had been added or removed — nothing has —

**Proposed:** as though the doxology were absent from one of these editions

Matching source locators will identify these exact local verses. These edits do not introduce manuscript witnesses or a direction-of-change claim.
