### A. Public title

**Romans 16:25–27: The Same Ending in Two Places**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Romans closes with a doxology — to the God who is able to strengthen you, to the only wise God be glory through Jesus Christ. Every edition compared here contains it. What they disagree about is where it belongs.

The Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Nestle's 1904 Greek edition and Boyd's Greek Textus Receptus print it at the very end, as 16:25–27 [S1, S2, S4, S5, S7]. The Majority Standard Bible and the Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine text place it earlier, straight after 14:23, where it is numbered 14:24–26 [S3, S6, C2]. Nothing is added and nothing is dropped; the same three verses stand in a different position.

This is a different kind of difference from most in this collection, and it is worth naming as such. Elsewhere the question is whether a sentence belongs in the text at all. Here it is a question of placement. BSB adds a footnote at 16:27 reporting that some manuscripts put the passage after Romans 14:23 or after Romans 15:33 [S1].

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

Because the words are the same, nothing in the doxology's content is in question. What placement changes is what the doxology closes. At the end of chapter 16 it seals the whole letter, following the greetings and the final benediction. After 14:23 it falls at the end of Paul's argument about the weak and the strong, and the letter then continues for two more chapters.

A reader is entitled to find that odd, and it is fair to say that a three-verse doxology sitting mid-letter reads strangely to most eyes. But strangeness is not evidence, and neither is familiarity. That the position most of us grew up with feels right is a fact about us, not about Paul's manuscript.

What a teacher can say is that the doxology is Scripture in both arrangements, that its wording is not in dispute, and that where Paul placed it is a genuine question on which editions differ. What a teacher should not do is treat a placement difference as though something had been added or removed — nothing has — or offer an explanation of how the passage came to sit in two places, which would require evidence this comparison does not contain.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.** Keep three questions apart when comparing Bibles, because they get run together constantly. Does this edition contain these words? Does it translate them the same way? Does it put them in the same place? Most of the differences you will meet are the first kind. This one is the third, and it is the only pure example in this collection.

A related point: the verse numbers follow the text rather than fixing it. In the editions that move the doxology, it is not numbered 16:25–27 in a strange location — it is numbered 14:24–26, because the numbering was applied to a text arranged that way. Numbers were added in 1551 [C5], long after the arrangements they describe, and they adapt to whichever arrangement an editor adopts.
