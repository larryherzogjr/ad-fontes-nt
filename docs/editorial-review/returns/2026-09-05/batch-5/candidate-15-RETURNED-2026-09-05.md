### A. Public title

**Only Begotten God, or Only Begotten Son: John 1:18**

### B. Public prose — source observation

John's prologue closes by saying that no one has ever seen God, and that the one at the Father's side has made him known. The editions differ over how that one is named.

Nestle's 1904 Greek edition reads μονογενὴς Θεός — *only begotten God*. Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus read ὁ μονογενὴς υἱός — *the only begotten Son* [C1]. The English editions follow suit: the Berean Standard Bible has "the one and only Son, who is Himself God," the Berean Literal Bible "the only begotten God," while the Majority Standard Bible and Young's Literal Translation read "Son" [C2].

Both Berean editions footnote the difference, and each keeps it separate from a second, purely translational note about rendering the Greek idiom "in the Father's bosom" [C3]. Those are two different questions and the editions are right to separate them: one is about which Greek word John wrote, the other about how to put a Greek phrase into English.

Notice which way this one runs. The critical edition here carries the higher title.

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

That is worth dwelling on, because it cuts against what many readers expect. As Larry Herzog Jr. observes in *Ad Fontes – Volume One*, at this verse receiving the older reading means receiving a higher Christology rather than letting one go [C4]. A reader who has been told that modern editions dilute the deity of Christ will find, at John 1:18, an edition calling the Son *God* where the traditional text calls him *Son*.

Neither reading is thin. "Only begotten Son" is the language of John 3:16 and of the Nicene Creed. "Only begotten God" states the same Person's deity outright. The prologue has already said in verse 1 that the Word was God, and that sentence is identical in all seven editions [C5]; so whichever reading stands at verse 18, the chapter confesses the same thing about the same Person.

The church's confession does not rest on this word either. Lutherans confess with the whole church that the Son of God is true God, begotten of the Father from eternity, and true man born of the virgin Mary — the Nicene Creed's article on the Son, taken up in Augsburg Confession III [C6]. That confession is drawn from the whole witness of Scripture. It is not established by John 1:18 reading one way, and it would not be endangered by its reading the other.

Which word John wrote is a real question, and comparing seven printed editions does not answer it.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.** Two habits are worth forming here.

The first: check which way a variant actually runs before assuming. It is easy to carry a rule of thumb — older manuscripts trim, traditional texts expand — and this verse breaks it. Rules of thumb are worth less than looking.

The second: notice when a publisher keeps its notes separate. At this verse both Berean editions give you one note about the Greek word and another about English idiom. That is a courtesy, and it lets you see that two unrelated questions happen to meet in the same sentence. Not every edition does it.
