### A. Public title

**The Refrain in Mark 9 That Some Bibles Print Three Times**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Start with the sentence every edition prints. At Mark 9:48, in all seven editions compared here, stands the warning about the worm that does not die and the fire that is not quenched [C2]. That is the fixed point. The question this note answers is how many times it appears before it.

In the Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus it appears three times: after the saying about the hand, at verse 44; after the saying about the foot, at verse 46; and again at verse 48 [S3, S4, S6, S7]. In the Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible and Nestle's 1904 Greek edition it appears once only, at verse 48, and there is no verse 44 or 46 [S1, S2, S5]. Where 44 and 46 are printed, their wording is identical to 48 — the same Greek in the Greek editions, the same English in the English ones [C2]. BSB footnotes the difference at verse 43, MSB at verse 44, and both cite Isaiah 66:24 as the source of the language [S1, S3].

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

Because the sentence is present either way, nothing about the warning itself is softened or strengthened by the difference. What changes is its shape. Printed three times, it works as a refrain: each amputation saying lands on the same terrible clause, and the repetition does the pressing. Printed once, the three sayings build and the clause falls at the end as a single closing blow. Preachers will have their preference. Neither is a different doctrine.

That is the whole of what can responsibly be said from this comparison. A teacher can show a class both arrangements and let them hear the difference in weight. A teacher cannot say, on this evidence, that scribes added the refrain for emphasis or that copyists dropped it as repetitive — those are historical claims about direction of change, and comparing seven printed editions cannot establish either. The identical wording at verse 48 is a fact; every story about how that came to be is a further claim needing further evidence, and this note does not offer one.

The same question arises again two verses later, at Mark 9:46.
