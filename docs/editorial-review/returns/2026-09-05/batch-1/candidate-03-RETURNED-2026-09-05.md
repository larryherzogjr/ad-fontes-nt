### A. Public title

**The Gap Between the Little Ones and the Lost Sheep**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Matthew 18:10 warns against despising one of these little ones. Matthew 18:12 asks what a man does when one sheep of a hundred goes astray. In the Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible and Nestle's 1904 Greek edition, those two verses sit side by side and there is no verse 11 [S1, S2, S5]. In the Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus, a sentence stands between them: that the Son of Man came to save the lost [S3, S4, S6, S7].

The editions that omit it do not conceal it. BSB prints it in a footnote attached to verse 10, describing it as the reading of the Byzantine text and the Textus Receptus; MSB, which has it in the text, footnotes that the critical text does not [S1, S3]. Both publishers point the reader to Luke 19:10 [S1, S3]. The disagreement is over a whole sentence in the Greek, not over how to translate one.

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

This is a case where the argument of the passage survives the difference intact. The parable that follows *is* the statement about seeking the lost, told rather than asserted: the shepherd leaves the ninety-nine and goes after the one. Where verse 11 is printed, that point is stated first and then illustrated. Where it is not, the illustration carries it alone. A reader is not left in doubt about what Matthew 18 teaches concerning the lost, in either edition.

Nor is the sentence itself in question anywhere. Both publishers cite Luke 19:10, where the same claim about the Son of Man stands — and it is worth being exact here: that verse is in every printed Bible in ordinary use, and nothing in this comparison touches it. What a teacher can responsibly say is that editions differ over whether Matthew states the point at this spot. What a teacher should not say is that anyone removed the gospel from Matthew, or that anyone inserted it. The comparison of printed editions shows the difference; it does not show which way the change ran, and this note does not claim to know.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.**

If you are comparing Bibles and find a number missing, the useful question is not "which publisher is trustworthy" but "where else does this sentence stand." Here the answer is easy: at Luke 19:10, in words no edition disputes. That is often, though not always, how these cases go. It is worth building the habit of asking it.
