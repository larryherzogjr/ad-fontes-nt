### A. Public title

**Mark 9:46, and Why the Answer Is the Same Twice**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Two verses after the question at Mark 9:44, the same thing happens again. The Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus print, after the saying about the foot, the same line about the worm and the fire, numbered 46 [S3, S4, S6, S7]. The Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible and Nestle's 1904 Greek edition do not, and run from verse 45 to verse 47 [S1, S2, S5]. BSB footnotes it at verse 45; MSB footnotes it at verse 46; both again cite Isaiah 66:24 [S1, S3].

The seven editions divide exactly as they did at verse 44, and no edition in this comparison prints one of the two without the other [C3]. The line is identical in wording to Mark 9:48, which every edition here prints [C2]. So, as at verse 44, no Bible on this list is short of the sentence; the editions differ over how many times it appears in the paragraph.

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

Since this is the same difference as at verse 44, the same limits apply, and a reader is owed the same short answer rather than a longer one dressed up as new information. Where verses 44 and 46 are printed, the warning recurs after each of the first two sayings and again at the end. Where they are not, it comes once, at verse 47's conclusion. The sayings themselves — hand, foot, eye — stand in all seven editions [C4].

The one thing this second occurrence adds is a caution about counting. It would be easy, comparing Bibles quickly, to treat Mark 9:44 and 9:46 as two separate losses and to feel the weight double. They are one difference occurring twice, and the editions handle them together. Adding up missing verse numbers as though each were an independent subtraction is one of the commonest ways of getting this subject wrong, and this paragraph is a clean place to see why. What no one can say from this comparison is which arrangement stood in Mark first; that requires evidence of a kind these seven editions do not supply.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.** If you are working through Mark 9 with a class, take verses 44 and 46 together in one pass rather than separately. Treating them as two discoveries suggests a pattern of loss that the evidence does not show — the same sentence, in the same paragraph, handled the same way by the same editions.
