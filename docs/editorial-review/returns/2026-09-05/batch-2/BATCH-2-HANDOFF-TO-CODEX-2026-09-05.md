## 2. New standing rulings from this batch

Three rulings were made during batch 2 that govern the rest of the queue, not just these files.

1. **Conceding an open question is permitted.** Where a difference cannot be resolved by pointing the reader to the same words elsewhere, the note says so plainly rather than manufacturing reassurance. Recorded in candidate-21's G. Most notes so far have been able to close reassuringly; when one cannot, it must not pretend otherwise.
2. **General transcriptional remarks are permitted; specific ones are not.** A note may describe how a *class* of sentence tends to behave in the manuscript tradition. It may **not** assert that a *particular* verse was added or dropped, or in which direction a change ran, without identified evidence and a fresh review decision. Recorded in candidate-10's D and G. This widens what draft-1 allowed; the second half is unchanged and still binds.
3. **Available catechetical connections may be declined.** Where a passage sits near a confessional locus but the textual difference does not bear on it, the note leaves the connection undrawn. Recorded in candidate-09's G. The Fifth Petition link at Mark 11:26 is deliberately absent, not overlooked.

## 3. Two integration items

Neither blocks import; both concern how footnotes are displayed.

- **candidate-10, BSB `MRK.15.note.117` and `MRK.15.note.118`** — two notes of different kinds share the anchor `MRK.15.27`: one a rendering alternative for "robbers," one a textual matter. Tag note kinds distinctly so a reader is not shown a translation choice and a textual difference as one undifferentiated footnote.
- **candidate-14, MSB `LUK.23.17.note.373`** — a single footnote combining a literal-rendering alternative with a textual statement. Split or tag the two components, so the "literally" clause is not read as part of the textual claim. This is the error §9's first checklist item exists to prevent.

## 4. A publisher reference that must not be corrected

**candidate-23, MSB `ROM.16.24.note.168`** ends: "some manuscripts place the text of verses of Romans 14:24–26 here or after Romans 15:33." Romans 14 has twenty-three verses, so this invites correction. **Make no correction.** When the doxology of 16:25–27 stands after 14:23, as it does in part of the tradition, those verses are conventionally numbered 14:24–26. The note uses a standard convention. This is recorded in candidate-23's R1 with the same instruction.
