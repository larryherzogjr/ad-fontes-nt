### A. Public title

**Mark 15:28 and a Prophecy Quoted Twice**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Mark 15:27 has two robbers crucified with Jesus, one on each side. Some Bibles add a sentence: that the Scripture was fulfilled which says, *and He was numbered with the transgressors*. The Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus print it as verse 28 [S3, S4, S6, S7]. The Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible and Nestle's 1904 Greek edition do not [S1, S2, S5].

The Berean editions do not bury it. BSB footnotes it at verse 27 as the reading of the Byzantine text and the Textus Receptus; MSB footnotes at verse 28 that the critical text lacks it; both point to Isaiah 53:12 and Luke 22:37 [S1, S3]. Two different kinds of footnote sit together at verse 27 in BSB, one about how to render "robbers" and one about this verse — worth not confusing, since only the second concerns what stood in the Greek [C4].

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

The prophecy is not at stake. Luke 22:37 stands in all seven editions, and there Jesus himself quotes the same line about being numbered with the transgressors — in the Greek editions, the identical clause that Mark 15:28 carries [C2]. So the New Testament's claim that Isaiah's words were fulfilled in the crucifixion is untouched by this difference.

What changes is who makes it, and when. Where Mark 15:28 is printed, the narrator stops the account to say that the Scripture was fulfilled, at the moment the two robbers are named. Where it is not, Mark reports the scene and leaves the connection unmade, while Luke has already recorded Jesus making it in advance, on the night he was betrayed. Both are ways of telling a reader that this was foretold; one is comment, the other is prediction.

A teacher can say that much and should stop there. Which arrangement Mark wrote is a question about the history of the text, and comparing seven printed editions does not answer it.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.** Fulfilment formulas — *this happened that the Scripture might be fulfilled* — are common in the Gospels and are among the places where editions most often differ, because a note in a margin explaining which prophecy a scene answers is the kind of thing that can end up in a text. That is a general observation about how such sentences behave, not a claim about this one. What can be said here is narrower and firmer: the same prophecy is quoted at Luke 22:37 in every edition on the list.

**Internal reviewer note — an unusually strong internal check.** C2 is not a resemblance but an identity: in RP2018 and TR-BOYD the Isaiah clause quoted at Luke 22:37 and the clause standing at Mark 15:28 are the same string. Both chapters are bundled, so this was verified rather than assumed.
