### A. Public title

**Acts 28:29 and a Sentence With No Parallel**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Paul's last recorded words to the Jewish leaders in Rome are at Acts 28:28: God's salvation has been sent to the Gentiles, and they will listen. Some Bibles then report the reaction — that when he had said this, the Jews went away, disputing sharply among themselves — before the account moves to Paul's two years in his own rented house. The Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus print that as verse 29 [S3, S4, S6, S7]. The Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible and Nestle's 1904 Greek edition do not, and go from verse 28 to verse 30 [S1, S2, S5].

BSB footnotes it at verse 28 as the reading of the Byzantine text and the Textus Receptus; MSB footnotes at verse 29 that the critical text lacks it [S1, S3]. Neither publisher offers a cross-reference. That is unusual: at comparable places in this collection both send the reader somewhere else in the New Testament where the disputed words also stand, and here neither does [C3].

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

That makes this the rarer case, and it deserves a straighter answer than the usual one. Elsewhere a reader can be told that the words in question stand somewhere else in every Bible on the list. Here no such pointer is on offer — not from either publisher, and not from the editions compared. If the sentence was Luke's, an edition without it loses a detail found nowhere else; if it was not, an edition with it carries one Luke did not write. Something genuinely turns on the question, even though the something is small.

What it is: a closing note on how Paul's declaration landed. With verse 29, Acts records the Jewish leaders leaving in sharp disagreement, and the argument continues off the page. Without it, verse 28 ends on Paul's statement and the narrative moves straight to two years of open preaching, so the last thing shown is not the reaction but Paul's continued welcome of all comers.

Neither arrangement changes what Acts reports about the mission to the Gentiles, which is stated in verse 28 in all seven editions [C2]. But a teacher should not tell a class that nothing is at stake here, because the honest position is that a detail either was or was not part of Luke's account, and this comparison does not establish which.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.** It is worth noticing when the usual reassurance is unavailable. Many differences between editions concern words that also stand elsewhere, and pointing there settles most of the worry. This one does not work that way. Saying so plainly is better than manufacturing a comfort the evidence does not supply — and noticing the difference between the two kinds of case is most of what careful reading in this area amounts to.
