### A. Public title

**Mark 11:26 and the Warning That Is Still There**

### B. Public prose — source observation

Mark 11:25 tells you to forgive when you stand praying, so that your Father in heaven will forgive you. Some Bibles follow it with the negative half: if you do not forgive, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses. The Majority Standard Bible, Young's Literal Translation, Robinson–Pierpont and Boyd's Textus Receptus print that as verse 26 [S3, S4, S6, S7]. The Berean Standard Bible, the Berean Literal Bible and Nestle's 1904 Greek edition do not, and run from verse 25 to verse 27 [S1, S2, S5].

Both Berean editions flag it. BSB's footnote sits at verse 25 and attributes the sentence to the Byzantine text and the Textus Receptus; MSB's sits at verse 26 and says the critical text lacks it. Both send the reader to Matthew 6:15 [S1, S3]. The editions disagree about the Greek here, not about how to translate it — the two Greek editions in the comparison that print verse 26 and the two that do not are divided on whether the sentence stood in Mark [C1].

### C. Public prose — local significance and interpretation

Follow the cross-reference and the worry dissolves. Matthew 6:15 — but if you do not forgive men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive yours — stands in all seven editions compared here, without a footnote anywhere [C2]. The warning is not missing from anyone's New Testament. What differs is whether Mark repeats at 11:26 what Matthew records in the Sermon on the Mount.

Notice too that verse 25 already carries the substance. It ties your forgiving to your being forgiven; verse 26 states the same tie from the other side. A reader whose Bible stops at verse 25 has been told the thing the missing verse would tell him.

That is as far as the evidence reaches. A teacher can show both arrangements and point to Matthew 6. A teacher cannot say from this comparison that the sentence was carried into Mark from Matthew, or dropped out of Mark by accident — those are claims about which way a change ran, and seven printed editions cannot settle them.

### D. Optional further detail / teacher note

**Proposed for readers.** The reflex, when a verse about forgiveness goes missing, is to assume something uncomfortable has been quietly removed. The check that answers it takes a minute: find where else the sentence stands. Here it stands at Matthew 6:15, in every edition, unmarked. Making that check a habit is worth more than any single verdict about a single verse.

**Internal reviewer note — a check that could actually be run.** Most cross-references in this queue point outside the thirty bundled chapters and can only be taken from the publishers or from general knowledge. Matthew 6 is bundled, so C2 is a direct observation of all seven releases rather than a borrowed claim. Where that is possible it should be done; it is the difference between a note that repeats a footnote and a note that verifies one.

**Internal reviewer note — a nearby confessional hook, deliberately not taken.** Mark 11:25 and Matthew 6:15 stand behind the Fifth Petition, and a note on forgiveness could easily be turned toward the catechism. It has not been. The textual difference does not touch the petition, the petition does not depend on Mark 11:26, and §3B warns against forcing an application.
