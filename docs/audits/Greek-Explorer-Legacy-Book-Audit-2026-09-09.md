# Greek Word Explorer: legacy book-language audit

Date: September 9, 2026 · AFNT-104 / AFNT-107

## Finding

At least **107 of 250 entries (42.8%)** contain identifiable wording that assumes the reader is following an original book or numbered study series. This is a confirmed entry count, not a count of every individual sentence requiring revision.

The first two volumes account for **99 of their 100 entries**. Monogenes was the sole original two-volume entry without an identified book-format reference in its body. The other 143 entries are not certified free of every possible contextual dependency; this audit identifies explicit book/chapter/volume/session framing.

## Scope and method

- Read all 250 Markdown entries under `/Users/lherzog/Documents/Codex/larryherzogjr/content/greek/`, excluding the collection landing page `_index.md`.
- Compared every source file byte-for-byte with `sources/om-studies/om-studies-2026-09-08-v2/raw/`: **250 identical files, zero differences**. Thus the same article prose is present in the app's selected content release, shared by ad-fontes.app and the desktop beta.
- Excluded YAML front matter and HTML comments from the body-text count. Historical `source`, `source_part` and `volume` fields are provenance, not automatically reader-facing errors. The current local Greek-page template uses category-based headings/navigation rather than rendering those source fields as the main heading.
- Searched chapter/book/volume/part wording, numbered/abbreviated chapter references, next/previous transitions, numbered sessions and book-title references. Reviewed contexts to distinguish the original publication from Scripture or cited works.
- Reviewed one explicit positive witness for each of the 107 entries listed below. Representative public pages Artos, Amen and Epiphaneia were fetched directly and confirmed HTTP 200 with the cited wording on September 9. This was not a fresh full-site crawl of all 250 public pages.
- No article text, approval record, corpus release, application behavior or deployed site was changed by this audit.

## Examples and significance

- **Artos**, lines 30 and 73: describes itself as part of a four-chapter Supper sequence and directs readers to “Chapter 43 of this book.” Standalone readers have neither that sequence nor that chapter number.
- **Hypostasis**, line 162: describes Part VII and the final chapter of the book, with its beginning and ending arranged around Logos and Parousia.
- **Amen**, line 194: “We have studied a hundred words across two volumes.” The Explorer now contains 250 entries, and visitors do not necessarily read them sequentially.
- **Epiphaneia**: refers to the beginning/end of its source book and to Sessions One, Six and Ten. This reveals a second source of inherited framing beyond Just Enough Greek.
- **Mesites**: links Anthropos correctly, but locates the discussion in Just Enough Greek, Volume Two. The link can remain while the unnecessary volume framing is revised.

There are several types of cleanup: explicit chapter/volume navigation, article self-description as a chapter, book-part ordering and opening/closing prose, and study-session references. A blanket replacement of “chapter” would damage valid Scripture references.

Examples excluded as legitimate Scripture context include Anastrophe (1 Peter chapters 1–2), Authenteo (1 Timothy chapter 3), Katapausis (Hebrews 3–4), Naos (1 Corinthians 3), Poterion (the preceding chapter of 1 Corinthians), and Soter (Titus chapters 1–3). Authenteo and Soter are included below for separate study-session references, not for their Bible citations. References to the Book of Concord, biblical books, other cited books and Christ's session at God's right hand are not counted as legacy navigation.

## Recommended follow-up

Edit the author-site articles as the source of truth. Replace legacy numbered references with named links to the relevant Greek entries; rewrite introductions/conclusions to work independently; retain meaningful historical provenance. Do not merely substitute the current word count for the old one or mechanically replace every “chapter.” Preserve Scripture, citations and substantive teaching while reviewing contextual edits.

After approval of changed prose, publish the author site and create a **new** immutable OM snapshot, retaining the current release. Verify the shared web/desktop article build, deploy the website and produce refreshed desktop packages. The existing downloaded desktop betas will retain their bundled prose until replaced; fixing the author website alone cannot update them.
