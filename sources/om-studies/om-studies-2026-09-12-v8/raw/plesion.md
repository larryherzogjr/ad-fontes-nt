The lawyer asks Jesus who counts as his neighbor. The parable's ending turns the question toward becoming a neighbor to the person who has been hurt. Love is not helped by endlessly refining the list of people we may safely ignore.

## The Word

**πλησίον** (*plēsion*) can mean *near*. Used substantivally with the article, it means *neighbor*. In that use it identifies another person, not necessarily someone whose house happens to share a boundary with ours.

The familiar English noun can hide the word's wider grammatical use. John 4:5 uses it in the geographical sense of being near. The commandments and the Good Samaritan account use it for the person toward whom love is owed.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew 22:39 gives the command to love the neighbor as oneself. Romans 13:9–10 connects that love with commandments against adultery, murder, theft, and coveting. Luke 10 places the question within a concrete account of mercy across a hostile social boundary.

The vocabulary does not turn love into an undefined warm feeling. The passages identify actions that protect another person's life, marriage, possessions, and well-being. What the neighbor needs becomes important.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus asks which man:

> “‘Which of these three do you think was a neighbor to the man who fell into the hands of robbers?’” (Luke 10:36, BSB)

The answer points to the one who showed mercy. The Samaritan's care includes approaching the wounded man, tending him, arranging lodging, and paying for help. His compassion takes a practical form.

The lawyer's earlier desire to justify himself also matters. The command to love is not a manageable test by which a sinner can confidently establish his own righteousness before God. Jesus exposes the inadequacy of the boundaries we use to make that claim easier.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The law identifies love's obligations and exposes our failure to meet them. The gospel gives forgiveness through Christ, whose saving work is not the reward for having loved sufficiently. Augsburg Confession VI then gives works their proper place as the fruit of faith.

The Small Catechism makes the neighbor's good concrete in its explanations of the commandments. We are to help protect bodily life, possessions, and reputation, not merely avoid the most obvious outward offense. Love notices what can be done for another.

Vocation helps direct that attention. We cannot personally meet every need in the world, but we have particular responsibilities and particular opportunities. Caring for the people entrusted to us is not inferior to imagining a more dramatic service somewhere else.

## Where People Get It Wrong

The parable can become a self-congratulatory story about how compassionate we would have been. Its setting should unsettle that confidence. It can also become so thoroughly allegorized that the actual wounded neighbor disappears from view.

Reading Scripture in relation to Christ need not erase the moral demand Jesus explicitly makes. The Savior who forgives our lovelessness also calls us to show mercy.

## So What

Notice one need you are in a position to meet. Give the help rather than waiting for an occasion that feels spiritually impressive.

When the command exposes how often you have passed by, confess that sin and receive Christ's forgiveness. Then return to the neighbor with hands freed for service rather than busy constructing a case for your own righteousness.
