God does not merely inspect a world that exists independently of him. He creates and sustains it. When Paul speaks of Christians being created in Christ Jesus, that familiar language of God's giving also describes the new life from which their good works proceed.

## The Word

**κτίζω** (*ktizō*) means *to create*. It is a verb, related to the noun **κτίσις** (*ktisis*), creation. The context identifies what is created and how the statement functions in the passage.

The same verb can describe the making of all things and the new life of believers. Sharing a verb does not erase the distinction between creation and redemption; it identifies God as the giver in both.

## Range of Meaning

Colossians 1:16 uses *ktizō* for all things created in, through, and for the Son. The surrounding passage places him before all things and says they hold together in him. He is not simply another item within the creation being described.

Revelation 4:11 praises God as creator. First Timothy 4:3 uses the verb for foods God created to be received with thanksgiving; verse 4 uses the related noun *ktisma*. God's gifts are not made unclean by treating ordinary bodily life as beneath religious attention.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul describes believers as:

> “For we are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance as our way of life.” (Ephesians 2:10, BSB)

The preceding verses exclude works as the cause of salvation and identify salvation as God's gift. Verse 10 then gives good works their place: they belong to the life God creates, rather than purchasing it.

Ephesians 4:24 also uses creation language for the new person, characterized by righteousness and holiness. Renewal is more than adopting a better public image. God's work reaches the life from which conduct comes.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Small Catechism's First Article explanation, II.1–2, confesses both God's creation and his continuing preservation of body and soul. It describes daily provision as fatherly goodness and mercy, received without our merit. Thanksgiving and service answer that giving.

Ephesians gives a corresponding order concerning new life in Christ: gift first, then the works for which God has made us. We should not collapse the catechism's articles into one another, but we can recognize the generosity of the same God throughout them.

This makes vocation a fitting place for gratitude. Feeding a child, making something useful, or caring for someone in need uses created gifts in service of a neighbor. These works matter without becoming the price of justification.

It also rules out despising the body or the ordinary necessities that sustain it. The creator gives them; the redeemed person receives and uses them under his Word.

## So What

Thank God for the life and abilities you have received, including the ordinary help that keeps you alive. Then ask how those gifts can serve the people before you.

When you fail, return to Christ's forgiveness rather than trying to create yourself anew by effort alone. [Kainē Ktisis](/greek/kaine-ktisis/) follows the related language of new creation and the hope grounded in God's action.
