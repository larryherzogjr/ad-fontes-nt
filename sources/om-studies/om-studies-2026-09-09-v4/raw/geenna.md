Jesus speaks seriously about judgment and tenderly about the Father's care in the same discourse. His warning is not evidence that compassion has disappeared. It teaches his disciples what to fear and whom to trust when human threats become frightening.

## The Word

**γέεννα** (*geenna*) names *Gehenna*, commonly translated *hell*. The name comes through Semitic forms associated with the Valley of Hinnom near Jerusalem. Its New Testament use belongs to the language of judgment.

That geographical background does not make Jesus' warnings mere travel directions. The warning must be understood from what Jesus says about judgment, rather than reduced to the physical features of a place outside the city.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew uses the word in Jesus' warnings about anger, sin, and judgment. Mark 9 connects it with severe warnings about causing offense and with language of unquenchable fire. James 3:6 uses it in describing the destructive power of the tongue.

[Hadēs](/greek/hades/) is a different Greek word, with its own connection to the realm of the dead. Translating both words as *hell* in some English contexts must not erase their distinctions or make every statement about death identical to a statement about final punishment.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus tells his disciples to:

> “Do not be afraid of those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Instead, fear the One who can destroy both soul and body in hell.” (Matthew 10:28, BSB)

The first part of Matthew 10:28 contrasts human killers, whose power is limited, with the one who has authority over both body and soul. The following verses speak of sparrows under the Father's care and the disciples' greater value.

The warning therefore belongs within Christ's preparation of his messengers for persecution. It does not invite fascination with punishment. It redirects fear away from human power and toward God, whose care they are also taught to trust.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Augsburg Confession XVII confesses Christ's return to judgment, the resurrection of all the dead, eternal life for believers, and eternal punishment of the ungodly and the devil. The Church cannot replace that teaching with a preferred picture in which judgment never finally occurs.

At the same time, the warning is spoken by the Savior who gives himself for sinners. Proclaiming judgment faithfully requires proclaiming the escape he provides. Hell is not the subject of Christian hope; Christ is.

The reference to both body and soul also prevents reducing final judgment to a purely inward feeling. Human life belongs to God's authority in its entirety. The salvation Christ gives likewise reaches the whole person, including bodily resurrection.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Speculative descriptions can go far beyond what Jesus says and turn preaching into an attempt to frighten people through invented detail. Silence about judgment is not the remedy. Restraint and fidelity to the actual words are.

Nor should human persecution be treated as the ultimate power over a Christian's future. Matthew places that fear beneath the Father's authority and care.

## So What

Take Christ's warning seriously, and take his promise seriously too. Confess your sins and hear the forgiveness he has secured for you.

When threats make the world seem ruled by those who can harm the body, return to the whole passage. The Father who knows the sparrows has not forgotten his children.
