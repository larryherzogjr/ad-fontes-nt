The Keys include a word people would often rather avoid: binding. Christ's Church must not call persistent refusal to repent harmless. Yet that difficult word belongs to a ministry seeking the sinner's restoration, not to a contest for control.

## The Word

**δέω** (*deō*) means *to bind* or *tie*. It can describe literal restraint or, in the sayings about the Keys, binding in relation to sin and the kingdom.

Greek also has similar-looking forms associated with need or necessity. A concordance result should therefore be checked in its actual sentence. The binding in Matthew 16:19 and 18:18 is not derived from the impersonal expression commonly translated *it is necessary*.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew 14:3 describes John being bound and imprisoned. Matthew 12:29 speaks of binding a strong man before plundering his house. Those are different contexts from the paired binding and loosing in the Church's commission.

In Matthew 18, the preceding instructions concern a brother's sin, speaking to him, witnesses, and telling the Church. This is not a free-floating power to constrain whatever a leader dislikes. The passage supplies a process and a purpose.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus says:

> “Truly I tell you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.” (Matthew 18:18, BSB)

The corresponding clause concerns release. The BSB represents the future perfect construction with *will have been bound*. That form deserves attention, but the grammar should not be made to establish an independent heavenly endorsement of every church decision.

The authority rests in Christ's words and is exercised under them. Matthew 18:15 identifies gaining the brother as the desired outcome. The increasingly public steps follow refusal to listen; they are not an invitation to begin with humiliation.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Smalcald Articles III.VII identify binding and loosing as the Church's authority received from Christ. Augsburg Confession XII insists that absolution must be available to those who repent after falling into sin. Together these confessions rule out both indifference to impenitence and refusal to forgive the repentant.

Binding tells the person who clings to sin that forgiveness cannot be treated as approval of that refusal. It calls for repentance. It does not claim that Christ's atonement has become insufficient or that return to him is impossible.

The pastoral aim remains restoration. Leaders and congregations are themselves accountable to God's Word, and accusations require truthfulness. Church discipline cannot rightly serve as retaliation against someone who reports wrongdoing or refuses an unlawful demand.

## Where People Get It Wrong

One error makes discipline an expression of personal anger. Another avoids every difficult word in the name of kindness. Neither serves a neighbor whose sin is destroying faith and harming others.

We also should not treat an erroneous human judgment as God's final verdict. Christ authorizes his Church to act according to his Word; he does not promise that its officers become incapable of error.

## So What

Receive a faithful warning as a call back to Christ. If you must address another person's sin, seek that person's good and speak with the humility of someone who also needs forgiveness.

Then keep the companion key in view. [Lyō](/greek/lyo/) concerns the release Christ commands his Church to give.
