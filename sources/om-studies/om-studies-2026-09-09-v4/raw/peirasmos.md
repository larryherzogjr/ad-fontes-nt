Jesus teaches us to ask our Father for protection in temptation. That prayer assumes both a real danger and a Father to whom we can turn. It does not suggest that God is waiting for an opportunity to entice his children into sin.

## The Word

**πειρασμός** (*peirasmos*) means *testing*, *trial*, or *temptation*. Context determines whether the emphasis falls on a hardship that tests faith or an enticement to do evil.

Those experiences can meet in the same situation. Suffering may become an occasion for despair or unbelief. Yet the hardship and the sinful response must not simply be identified with each other.

## Range of Meaning

James 1:2 speaks of various trials through which endurance develops. Later, James uses the related verb **πειράζω** (*peirazō*) when he insists that God tempts no one to evil (1:13). He then locates enticement in our own desires.

First Peter 1:6–7 likewise describes faith being tested through suffering. These passages do not make pain pleasant or the sufferer guilty merely for finding it difficult. They teach that suffering need not have the last word about faith's future.

In Matthew 26:41, Jesus tells his disciples to watch and pray against entering temptation. Their willingness does not remove their weakness. Prayer expresses dependence on help they need.

## Where You’ll Meet It

The Lord's Prayer includes this petition:

> “And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.” (Matthew 6:13, BSB)

First Corinthians 10:13 promises God's faithfulness amid trial and temptation. The surrounding warnings concern falling into sin, especially idolatry. Paul directs the Corinthians to flee idolatry, not to test how close they can come to it without falling.

The promised way out enables endurance. It need not mean that every difficult circumstance immediately disappears. God's faithfulness is the ground for resisting temptation rather than surrendering to it as unavoidable.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Small Catechism's explanation of the Sixth Petition begins by affirming that God tempts no one. We ask him to preserve us against the devil, the world, and our flesh, which would draw us into false belief, despair, and shameful conduct.

The explanation also acknowledges that these enemies attack. Praying the petition does not mean that a temptation will never be felt. It asks our Father to keep us through the assault and bring us to victory.

## Where People Get It Wrong

The familiar assurance that God will never give someone more than he can handle turns 1 Corinthians 10:13 into a different promise. Paul himself describes affliction beyond his strength, through which he learned to rely on the God who raises the dead (2 Corinthians 1:8–9).

A person overwhelmed by suffering has not thereby disproved God's faithfulness or failed a test of personal capacity. The promise directs us to God's help against temptation. It does not tell the exhausted Christian that needing help is evidence of deficient faith.

## So What

Pray for preservation before you feel strong enough to resist. Take the escape that is available, seek help, and avoid situations that draw you toward sin.

When suffering overwhelms you, let others carry part of the burden. Your Father has not required self-sufficiency as the condition of his care. The petition places your weakness before the one whose faithfulness is greater than your strength.
