Jesus warns against gaining the world while losing one's life. He also distinguishes the soul from the body when telling disciples whom to fear. The word translated *soul* must be read with enough care to preserve both sayings.

## The Word

**ψυχή** (*psychē*) can mean *life*, *soul*, or the person considered as a living self. No single English rendering fits every occurrence equally well.

The range does not authorize us to choose whichever meaning suits a preferred theory of human nature. Each passage identifies the contrast or relationship that matters there.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew 16:25–26 uses *psychē* for the life a person tries to save or loses for Christ's sake. The BSB renders it *life* in verse 25 and *soul* in verse 26, showing how context shapes the English choice. Luke 12:19–20 likewise uses the word in the rich man's self-address and God's demand for his life.

Matthew 10:28, however, distinguishes body and soul. Hebrews 4:12 speaks of soul and spirit within its description of God's penetrating word, while 1 Thessalonians 5:23 prays for the person's complete holiness using spirit, soul, and body.

These texts should not be forced into a diagram by assuming every list serves the same purpose. Their concerns include judgment, God's knowledge of us, and his preservation of the whole person.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus says:

> “Do not be afraid of those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Instead, fear the One who can destroy both soul and body in hell.” (Matthew 10:28, BSB)

He continues by directing fear toward the one able to destroy both soul and body in hell. The passage recognizes a distinction while refusing to place any part of the person beyond God's authority.

Philippians 1:23 speaks of Paul's desire to depart and be with Christ. First Corinthians 15 announces the resurrection of the body. The Christian hope includes both being with Christ after death and the future raising of the dead.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The body is God's creation and belongs within his redemption. The creed's confession of bodily resurrection prevents us from treating embodied life as an unfortunate prison from which salvation finally releases the real person.

That does not require denying the soul or denying personal life with Christ between death and resurrection. The Lord's care is not suspended when bodily death occurs. The promised resurrection brings the completion for which Christians wait.

The aim is to confess the whole scriptural promise without setting one part against another. We can commend the departed to Christ and care reverently for the body, trusting the Lord who made and redeemed the person we love.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Translating *psychē* as *soul* everywhere can obscure ordinary references to life. Translating it as *life* in a way that erases Matthew 10's distinction creates the opposite problem.

Popular language that treats the body as disposable falls short of Christian hope. But blaming every affirmation of the soul on Greek philosophy would also fail to hear Jesus' own words.

## So What

Care for bodily life as a gift, including the bodies of people whose strength or abilities have diminished. Their worth does not depend on what they can presently do.

At death, entrust the person to Christ and await the resurrection he promises. [Koimaomai](/greek/koimaomai/) explains the sleep image that Scripture uses within this hope.
