A rescue story often begins with someone barely able to hold on. Help arrives, a hand reaches out, and the endangered person supplies the last necessary movement. That can be a moving picture of rescue. It is not the picture Paul uses to explain our conversion.

Paul says we were dead. Then he names the one who made us alive.

## The Word

**νεκρός** (*nekros*) means *dead*. It is an adjective and can also be used to speak of a dead person or the dead collectively. The same word appears in accounts of bodily resurrection and in Paul's description of people living in sin.

That second use is not a claim that unbelievers have no thoughts, emotions, or choices. Ephesians 2 describes the spiritually dead as actively following desires and patterns of disobedience. The death concerns their condition before God, not the absence of biological or mental activity.

## Range of Meaning

A word used literally in one passage can be used figuratively in another without losing its force. Spiritual death is not physical unconsciousness, but neither is it a mild inconvenience. The sinner lacks the life that only God can give.

We therefore should not derive the whole doctrine of conversion from everything a corpse cannot do. Paul's sentence supplies the decisive relationship: people dead in sins are made alive with Christ by the merciful action of God.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Ephesians 2:1 begins with the hearers' death in offenses and sins. Verses 4–5 identify God, rich in mercy, as the one who makes them alive with Christ. Verses 8–10 then speak of grace, faith, and the good works for which they are newly created.

Colossians puts the diagnosis and the remedy in one sentence:

> “When you were dead in your trespasses and in the uncircumcision of your sinful nature, God made you alive with Christ. He forgave us all our trespasses,” (Colossians 2:13, BSB)

John 5:24–25 joins hearing Christ's word, believing, and passing from death into life. His voice is not merely advice shouted toward people who can already supply the life he offers. The Son speaks, and the dead hear and live.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Formula of Concord attributes conversion and faith entirely to the Holy Spirit's work, not partly to an independent contribution from the unconverted will. It distinguishes ordinary outward capacities from the new heart and understanding the Spirit creates (FC SD II.25–26).

The Formula also explains what passivity in conversion means. God works through the proclaimed and heard Word, and he renews the person's understanding and will. Conversion is not like carving an unaware figure from stone. The person truly hears and believes, but the origin of that faith is God's gracious work (FC SD II.89).

This preserves the force of the diagnosis without turning the convert into a lifeless object afterward. God makes living believers who trust Christ and begin to serve their neighbors.

## Where People Get It Wrong

The half-drowned sinner illustrates a person with some remaining capacity to complete the rescue. Applied to conversion, it can make the decisive difference a movement supplied independently of grace. Paul's account instead gives thanks to God for the life that was absent.

But we should not answer by saying people never resist God, never hear the gospel, or never exercise faith. Those are real features of the biblical account. The distinction concerns the source of saving faith. The renewed will believes because the Spirit has made it new.

## So What

Your hope does not rest on having contributed the one sound movement that distinguished you from another sinner. It rests on Christ, whose word gives life and whose forgiveness is for the ungodly.

Hear that word again when faith is weak. [Doulos](/greek/doulos/) names the bondage from which he frees you; [Zōopoieō](/greek/zoopoieo/) names his act of making alive. The severity of the diagnosis serves the greatness of the gift.
