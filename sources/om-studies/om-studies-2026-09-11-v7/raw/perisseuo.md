Paul says that grace “increased all the more” where sin increased (Romans 5:20, BSB). The next question is predictable: should we keep sinning so grace can increase? His answer does not retract the abundance of grace. It directs Christians to their Baptism into Christ's death and the new life that follows.

## The Word

**περισσεύω** (*perisseuō*) means *to abound* or *to overflow*. It can describe having plenty, increasing generosity, or an abundance of love.

Romans 5:20 uses the intensified compound **ὑπερπερισσεύω** (*hyperperisseuō*). The BSB renders it *increased all the more*. This is a related compound, not another occurrence of the simple verb.

## Range of Meaning

Philippians 4:12 uses the simple verb for having abundance, alongside Paul's experience of need. Verse 18 describes his having plenty after receiving the Philippians' gift. Neither sentence promises that believers will always be materially prosperous.

Second Corinthians 9:8 speaks of grace overflowing so that believers may overflow in good works. First Thessalonians 3:12 asks the Lord to make them abound in love. Abundance can describe what is received and the service it makes possible.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul writes:

> “The law came in so that the trespass would increase; but where sin increased, grace increased all the more,” (Romans 5:20, BSB)

The surrounding argument contrasts Adam's trespass with Christ's saving work. Grace does more than barely balance the damage. Through Christ it reigns in righteousness leading to eternal life.

Romans 6:1–4 then refuses the conclusion that sin should be encouraged. Those baptized into Christ have been joined to his death and resurrection. Continuing under sin's rule contradicts the new life God has given.

The order matters. Paul does not make grace smaller to protect moral seriousness. He explains what the abundant gift has done to the people who receive it.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The gospel can provoke the question whether free forgiveness makes obedience unnecessary. Paul's own argument anticipates it. That does not make every accusation of moral laxity proof that a preacher has taught correctly, but it warns against protecting good works by making forgiveness depend on them.

Baptism supplies Paul's answer because it joins the believer to Christ's saving death and risen life. New obedience grows from that gift. It is not an extra charge attached after grace has been advertised as free.

The same abundance moves outward toward the neighbor. In 2 Corinthians 9, God's provision equips believers to give. In 1 Thessalonians 3, the Lord causes love to increase. The giver remains the source of the life and service he calls forth.

## So What

You do not need to make your sin look small so that God's grace will seem large enough to cover it. Confess it truthfully and hear the greater promise in Christ.

Nor do you need to keep your neighbor at a distance while deciding whether enough mercy remains for you. The grace that forgives also gives a new life of love. [Dōrean](/greek/dorean/) considers the freeness of that gift; its abundance gives you reason to receive it with confidence and serve with generosity.
