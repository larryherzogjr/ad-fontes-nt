Jesus' final declaration in John is familiar in the words *It is finished*, which the BSB retains. John has been tracing the work the Father gave the Son to do; at the cross, Jesus declares its completion.

## The Word

**τελέω** (*teleō*) means *to finish*, *to complete*, or *to accomplish*. In John 19:30, the form is **τετέλεσται** (*tetelestai*), a perfect passive form: something has been brought to completion and stands completed.

The perfect form gives the declaration its completed character. It does not, by itself, name everything accomplished or supply an entire doctrine of atonement. The speaker and the surrounding Gospel tell us what this completion means.

## Range of Meaning

The verb can describe carrying something out or bringing it to its end. It can also mean paying, as with taxes in Romans 13:6. That use does not make *paid in full* the necessary translation wherever the verb appears.

Luke 12:50 uses it of the baptism of suffering Jesus must undergo. Luke 18:31 uses it of what the prophets wrote about the Son of Man being accomplished. John 4:34 and 17:4 use the related verb **τελειόω** (*teleioō*) for completing the Father's work. These passages illuminate the theme without all containing the same headword.

## Where You’ll Meet It

John reports Jesus' declaration:

> “When Jesus had received the sour wine, He said, ‘It is finished.’ And bowing His head, He yielded up His spirit.” (John 19:30, BSB)

Verse 28 has already used *tetelestai* to describe everything having been completed, while also speaking of Scripture's fulfillment. After receiving the sour wine, Jesus makes his declaration and gives up his spirit.

The point is more than the ending of pain. The Son who came to do the Father's will reaches the completion of his appointed work. The familiar story about this exact word being stamped on paid invoices is unnecessary to that reading; a commonly cited papyrus example was subsequently corrected to a different verb. John's own account provides firmer ground.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Christ's sacrifice needs no supplement from us. Hebrews 10:10–14 likewise contrasts repeated offerings with his one sufficient offering for sins. Our repentance and faith do not provide a missing portion of the price.

This does not make the resurrection, ascension, or Christ's continuing intercession unnecessary. His declaration is not a claim that nothing remains in God's saving purpose. It announces the accomplishment of the work brought to its appointed completion at the cross.

The gifts of forgiveness in the Word and Sacraments distribute the benefit of that accomplished salvation. They do not repeat Christ's death. Faith receives what he has done, and the Christian life follows from the gift.

## So What

A troubled conscience often tries to turn tomorrow's improvement into today's assurance. Christ directs you to a work already accomplished. Confess your sin, hear his forgiveness, and begin again from his promise.

There is much for love to do for the neighbor. There is nothing for your love to add to the sufficiency of Christ's offering. His completed work frees you to serve without treating each act as an installment on an unpaid debt.
