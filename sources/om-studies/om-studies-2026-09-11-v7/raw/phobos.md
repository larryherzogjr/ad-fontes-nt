Scripture tells believers not to fear, and Scripture tells believers to live in fear before God. Those statements are not resolved by making every occurrence mean the same thing. We must ask what is feared and how the person stands before God.

## The Word

**φόβος** (*phobos*) means *fear* and, in some settings, *reverence*. It can describe dread of punishment or the serious awe with which a creature regards God.

The distinction between servile and filial fear helps express the difference: the dread of a slave expecting condemnation and the reverence of a child who trusts his father. These are theological descriptions of the contexts, not two separate Greek words hidden inside the noun.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 8:15 contrasts slavery leading back into fear with the Spirit of adoption. Through the Spirit, believers call upon God as Father. Their access rests on what he has given them in Christ.

First Peter 1:17 directs those who address God as Father to live in reverence. The following verses ground their life in redemption through Christ's precious blood. Calling God Father does not make his holiness trivial.

Hebrews 12:28 also joins a received kingdom with reverent worship. It uses other Greek words for devotion and awe, so it is a related passage rather than another occurrence of *phobos*.

## Where You’ll Meet It

John writes:

> “There is no fear in love, but perfect love drives out fear, because fear involves punishment. The one who fears has not been perfected in love.” (1 John 4:18, BSB)

The preceding verse speaks of confidence on the day of judgment. John is not forbidding every serious awareness of God's majesty. He addresses the fear of punishment that God's love in Christ overcomes.

Philippians 2:12 uses *phobos* in the familiar expression “fear and trembling,” which the BSB retains. Verse 13 immediately identifies God as the one working in believers both desire and effort. Their serious obedience rests on His work, not on their independence from Him.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Small Catechism's explanations repeatedly join fearing God with loving him. The First Commandment adds trust: God is to be feared, loved, and trusted above everything. Reverence and confidence belong together.

The law exposes real sin and brings the conscience before God's judgment. The gospel announces forgiveness in Christ and gives confidence to approach the Father. It does not teach us that sin was harmless or that obedience no longer matters.

This distinction helps the frightened conscience receive the right word. Someone terrified that Christ's mercy cannot include him needs the promise addressed to sinners, not a demand to demonstrate perfect emotional calm.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Reducing fear to polite respect can empty the biblical language of its weight. God is holy, and his commandments are not optional advice.

The opposite error makes every remaining feeling of fear proof that faith is absent. John's words direct us toward God's prior love; they should not become another test by which an anxious Christian disqualifies himself from receiving it. Trust can cling to the promise while feelings remain unsettled.

## So What

Take God's Word seriously and bring your fear to him. You do not have to choose between honoring your Father and believing that he receives you for Christ's sake.

[Abba](/greek/abba/) follows the address his Spirit gives you. The one before whom you stand in reverence is the Father to whom your Savior teaches you to pray.
