God knows what will happen before it happens. Scripture also speaks of his gracious choosing of people in Christ. The Formula of Concord distinguishes these truths so that foreknowledge does not become an explanation that makes God the author of evil or turns election into a threat.

## The Word

**πρόγνωσις** (*prognōsis*) means *foreknowledge*. The noun appears in Acts 2:23 and 1 Peter 1:2. It describes God's prior knowledge within passages about Christ's death and the identity of God's people.

Romans 8:29 and 11:2 use the related verb **προγινώσκω** (*proginōskō*), *to foreknow*. They belong in the discussion, but they are not additional occurrences of the noun.

## Range of Meaning

Knowing beforehand and choosing are distinguishable concepts. That does not mean every biblical use of *foreknow* must describe a detached prediction. Romans speaks of people whom God foreknew, within an argument about his faithful saving purpose.

We must attend to that context instead of forcing the word into either a mechanical forecast or a complete definition of election. The confessional distinction between foreknowledge and election is a theological account of Scripture's teaching, not a claim that two dictionary entries resolve every passage.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Peter describes Jesus as handed over:

> “by the predetermined plan and foreknowledge of God” (Acts 2:23, NET)

In the same sentence, Peter holds those who crucified Jesus responsible. God's saving purpose does not turn their wrongdoing into innocence. The next verse proclaims the resurrection: their violence did not defeat God's purpose.

First Peter 1:1–2 addresses people chosen according to the Father's foreknowledge, through the Spirit's sanctifying work, for obedience and the sprinkling of Christ's blood. The passage locates their life within the saving action of Father, Son, and Spirit.

Romans 8:29–30 connects foreknowledge with predestination, calling, justification, and glorification. Romans 11:2 affirms that God has not rejected the people he foreknew. Both passages should be heard within their arguments about God's faithfulness.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration XI.4–8, distinguishes God's foreknowledge, which encompasses good and evil, from election, which concerns God's children and their salvation. Foreknowledge does not cause evil. Election is God's gracious provision for salvation, not merely his observation of what people will do.

This rules out the claim that God chooses people on the basis of foreseen faith, as though faith supplied a reason for his gracious choice. It also rules out treating his knowledge of sin as proof that he authors it.

The article directs us to Christ, the book of life, when considering election (XI.13). [Proorizō](/greek/proorizo/) considers predestination more closely. The pastoral direction is the same: seek assurance in the gospel's promise, rather than in an attempt to inspect God's hidden counsel.

## So What

Your future is not unknown to God. That truth need not turn every fear into a prediction about your final destiny.

Hear what he has actually made known: Christ died and rose for sinners, and his promise calls you to trust him. Foreknowledge gives you no warrant to excuse evil or despair of mercy. God's revealed gospel gives you reason to repent, believe, and entrust the future to him.
