It is natural to want the people we love to find Christian faith convincing. We try to answer questions clearly and remove misunderstandings. Paul shares the desire that people believe, but he will not make their expectations the standard by which the gospel is redesigned.

The crucified Christ remains at the center of his preaching.

## The Word

**μωρία** (*mōria*) means *foolishness*. In 1 Corinthians, Paul uses the word to expose the conflict between human judgments about wisdom and God's way of saving sinners.

The gospel appears foolish to those who reject it. Paul is not confessing that God's work is actually defective or that Christians should cultivate stupidity. He is showing how radically human judgment can misidentify God's wisdom.

## Range of Meaning

In 1 Corinthians 1:18, the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing. Verse 21 speaks of God's saving through the foolishness of preaching, and verse 23 identifies the proclaimed subject: Christ crucified.

In 2:14, the unbeliever regards the things of God's Spirit as foolishness and cannot receive them through unaided human discernment. In 3:19, the judgment is reversed: this world's wisdom is foolishness before God. The standard by which people dismiss the gospel is itself called into question.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul says:

> “God was pleased to save those who believe by the foolishness of preaching.” (1 Corinthians 1:21, NET)

Read that sentence with verse 23. The foolishness concerns the proclamation of a crucified Messiah, not a requirement that sermons be careless or incomprehensible. Paul reasons, explains, and appeals throughout the letter.

[Stauros](/greek/stauros/) names the cross at the heart of that proclamation. [Skandalon](/greek/skandalon/) considers its offense. The message contradicts the sinner's expectation that salvation will recognize or complete an achievement already begun within us.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Faith is created through the gospel by the Holy Spirit. Clear explanations serve that proclamation, but they do not give us control over its reception. The hearer's resistance to God's gift runs deeper than an unanswered question or an unfortunate choice of presentation.

That is why the church keeps preaching Christ crucified. We are not waiting for a strategy that can make the cross acceptable to the old self on its own terms. God's word brings the promise by which he creates faith.

Reason remains a gift with real work to do. We use it to understand language, test claims, answer objections, and speak intelligibly. It serves the Word faithfully when it listens rather than demanding that God save in a way it would have chosen.

## Where People Get It Wrong

The desire to communicate well becomes a problem when it quietly changes the message. Removing the need for forgiveness or turning Christ into the sponsor of our self-improvement may make the presentation easier to accept while abandoning what Paul preaches.

The opposite mistake is to treat hostility as proof of faithfulness. Confusion, arrogance, and bad arguments are not made holy because someone objects to them. The offense of the gospel does not excuse our carelessness.

## So What

Speak clearly to the people you love. Listen carefully and answer honestly. Their conversion is not a burden God has placed on your persuasive ability.

The word you share announces a Savior who was crucified for sinners. God is able to work through that apparently unimpressive message, just as he worked through the cross it proclaims.
