The cross is so familiar that we can recognize its outline without thinking about what happened on it. Paul will not let that familiarity determine its meaning. The cross he preaches is the place where Jesus was executed and where God acted to save sinners.

## The Word

**σταυρός** (*stauros*) means *cross*. In the New Testament's accounts of Jesus' death, it names the instrument of crucifixion. In Paul's preaching, it also names that death and its saving significance.

When Paul speaks of the message of the cross, the object and the event belong together. He is not announcing the spiritual power of a shape. He is proclaiming the crucified Christ.

## Range of Meaning

Philippians 2:8 names the cross as the depth of Christ's humiliation. Colossians 2:14 speaks of the record against us being removed and nailed to it. Galatians 6:14 makes the cross the Christian's boast, overturning the impulse to establish worth through impressive credentials.

Jesus also speaks of the disciple's cross. In Luke 9:23, taking it up belongs with denying oneself and following him. The disciple's suffering does not repeat Christ's atonement or supply something his death lacked. It belongs to following the Lord who gives life through his own death.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul writes:

> “For the message about the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God.” (1 Corinthians 1:18, NET)

The preceding verse warns against a style of preaching that empties the cross of its force. The following verses identify Christ crucified as the message that human expectations reject.

[Mōria](/greek/moria/) names its apparent foolishness. [Skandalon](/greek/skandalon/) names the offense. Both help us hear why Paul will not replace this proclamation with a more flattering account of human possibilities.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Luther's [Heidelberg Disputation](https://thebookofconcord.org/sources-and-context/heidelberg-disputation/) directs theology to God as he makes himself known through suffering and the cross. Theses 19–21 challenge the use of visible achievement as the measure of divine favor. The theologian of the cross must speak truthfully about sin, suffering, and God's saving work rather than making appearances the judge.

This does not make pain good in itself. It directs us to the crucified Son, whose apparent defeat is the place of our redemption. Nor does it forbid recognizing God's gifts in creation. It refuses to find saving certainty in an ascent from our strength or success to God's approval.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Calling every annoyance a cross can drain the word of its connection with discipleship. Yet chronic pain or an ordinary burden should not be dismissed as too small for God's care. The question is how suffering is borne in faith, not whether it looks dramatic enough.

A cross worn or displayed can confess Christ faithfully. The danger is forgetting the crucified Lord while retaining a pleasing symbol. The remedy is the message that tells us what he did there.

## So What

Your suffering does not pay for sin. Christ's death has done what your endurance cannot do. You can seek relief, receive help, and love your neighbor without turning hardship into a contribution toward salvation.

The cross directs you to the Savior who gave himself for you, even when your circumstances offer no visible evidence of triumph.
