A congregation has many activities, but Acts identifies a definite center to its common life: the apostles' teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayers. Its teaching is received before it is organized into programs.

## The Word

**διδαχή** (*didachē*) means *teaching* or *instruction*. The noun can refer to the activity of teaching or the content taught. It is closely related to [Didaskalia](/greek/didaskalia/), and their uses overlap.

It would be misleading to insist that one always means the teaching process and the other always means doctrine. The distinction has to be established from a particular sentence rather than imposed as a permanent rule between the two nouns.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew 7:28 describes the crowds' response to Jesus' teaching. John 7:16 connects his teaching with the Father who sent him. Romans 16:17 warns against divisions and obstacles contrary to the teaching believers have learned.

The noun also occurs in descriptions of false teaching. It does not guarantee truth simply by being present. The source and substance of the instruction are what require attention.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Acts says of the baptized believers:

> “They were devoting themselves to the apostles’ teaching” (Acts 2:42, NET)

The following phrases name fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayer. The passage presents teaching as part of a shared, continuing life. It is neither a private intellectual hobby nor a disposable stage before worship becomes meaningful.

Romans 6:17 describes believers' obedience from the heart to the pattern of teaching to which they were entrusted. Teaching belongs to their new life under grace, not merely to an examination they once passed.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Church receives its message from the apostles' witness to Christ. Its task is to confess that message faithfully, not to make novelty the test of vitality. Augsburg Confession V names the preaching of the gospel and the sacraments as God's means for giving the Spirit and faith.

That makes doctrine pastoral. What is taught about Christ, forgiveness, Baptism, and the Supper determines what a frightened sinner is told to trust. An error need not be emotionally harsh to take away comfort; it may sound reassuring while quietly moving assurance from Christ to human performance.

At the same time, fidelity is not proved by multiplying quarrels over every personal preference. Romans 16 speaks of departures from received teaching, not of granting every taste the status of doctrine. The Church needs both clarity about God's Word and restraint about what he has not commanded.

## Where People Get It Wrong

One mistake opposes doctrine to love, as though truthful teaching could not serve a neighbor. Another treats accurate vocabulary as permission to despise the people being taught. Acts presents teaching within a community of shared gifts and care.

The title of the early Christian work commonly called the *Didache* should also be distinguished from the New Testament word itself. Recognizing the title does not make that later document the referent of Acts 2:42.

## So What

Ask what your congregation's teaching gives you to trust when you have sinned or are afraid. Does it direct you to Christ's promise, or does it leave you measuring your own adequacy?

Then keep learning. [Katēcheō](/greek/katecheo/) follows the instruction by which that confession becomes familiar and usable.
