The creed names the Holy Spirit as the giver of life. We can repeat that description so often that it becomes a title without an action. The New Testament gives us the verb: God makes alive.

That is stronger than offering a better way to use a life we already possess. It names the giving of what the recipient lacks.

## The Word

**ζωοποιέω** (*zōopoieō*) means *to make alive* or *to give life*. It combines the language of life with making. Greek editions may print the word with an iota subscript, *ζῳοποιέω*; the verb is the same.

Its grammar directs attention to the giver and the one who receives life. In active forms, someone gives life. In passive forms, someone is made alive. That observation fits the passages, but grammar alone does not settle the whole doctrine of conversion. We must also ask who acts and what the context says about the recipient.

## Range of Meaning

The word can concern the resurrection of the dead or the life given by the Spirit. It is not a technical term reserved for one moment in a Christian's biography.

The related compound in Ephesians 2:5 and Colossians 2:13 adds the sense of being made alive *together with* Christ. [Nekros](/greek/nekros/) follows those passages from their starting point: the people God makes alive were dead in sins.

## Where You’ll Meet It

> “For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whomever he wishes.” (John 5:21, NET)

The Father gives life, and the Son gives life. John 6:63 speaks of the Spirit giving life and connects that life with the words Jesus speaks. The passages draw us toward God's action rather than a technique for manufacturing spiritual vitality.

First Corinthians 15:22 speaks of being made alive in Christ. Verse 45 calls the last Adam life-giving, within an argument about the resurrection body. Christ does not merely return to life for himself; he gives life to his people.

Second Corinthians 3:6 contrasts the letter that kills with the Spirit who gives life. The contrast belongs to Paul's account of the new covenant. It is not permission to abandon the written Scriptures in favor of an inward impression. First Peter 3:18 likewise uses the passive form in speaking of Christ being made alive.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The creed's description of the Spirit belongs to this biblical confession of God as life's giver. Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are not competing sources from which a Christian must choose.

God's life-giving work also reaches us through his Word. The Spirit is not an alternative to hearing Christ. He brings us to faith in the Christ whom the gospel proclaims, and he sustains that faith through God's gifts.

The resurrection promise therefore has a personal foundation. We await the Lord who gives life to the dead, not an impersonal force that might keep some part of us going.

## So What

When the church speaks the creed, hear an action in the Spirit's title. The God you confess gives life.

You do not have to produce the life before approaching his promise. Hear Christ's word, receive his gifts, and ask him to sustain you. The Lord who makes alive has not left his people to animate themselves.
