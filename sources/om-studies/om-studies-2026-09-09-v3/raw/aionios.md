Eternal life is a future hope, but Jesus also speaks of it as a present gift. The believer who hears his word does not wait until death to begin belonging to the life he gives. That present possession rests on the same Lord who will raise the dead.

## The Word

**αἰώνιος** (*aiōnios*) means *eternal* or *everlasting* in the passages considered here. It is an adjective related to **αἰών** (*aiōn*), age.

The relationship between the words does not allow us to determine the duration of every occurrence by etymology alone. We must read what the adjective describes and how the passage contrasts it with what is temporary.

## Range of Meaning

John 3:16 speaks of eternal life as the gift received through faith in God's Son. John 17:3 describes that life in terms of knowing the Father and Jesus Christ whom he sent.

Second Corinthians 4:17–18 contrasts present suffering and visible, temporary things with eternal glory and what is unseen. Matthew 25:46 applies the same adjective to punishment and life within one statement of judgment.

Knowing God is therefore central to eternal life, but duration is not irrelevant. The adjective must not be reduced to a special quality that could end when the gift's recipient dies.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus says that the one who hears and believes:

> “has eternal life and will not be condemned” (John 5:24, NET)

He then says that this person has crossed from death to life. The verbs identify a present gift with lasting consequences.

The same discourse also announces a coming hour when those in the tombs will hear his voice and come out. Present possession does not eliminate future bodily resurrection. The Lord who gives life now will bring his promise to completion.

Matthew 25's use of the adjective for both outcomes also prevents treating the warning of judgment as though the word itself meant only a brief corrective interval. Its force must be heard within Jesus' actual sentence.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Faith receives a life grounded in Christ's promise rather than in a successful prediction of one's remaining years. The gospel gives forgiveness and fellowship with God now; death does not make that gift expire.

This assurance is not permission to set Christ aside while retaining a possession detached from him. Jesus names hearing his word and believing the Father who sent him. The certainty lies in this trustworthy Savior, not in an independent spiritual asset we control.

Nor should the reality of judgment be softened into a mere difference of perspective. Scripture announces both salvation and condemnation. The church proclaims the warning seriously and directs sinners to the Son given for their salvation.

## So What

Hear eternal life as Christ's gift to you now, not only as a distant possibility. Return to the Word and sacraments through which he sustains faith in his promise.

When death approaches, the promise does not have to be invented afresh. The Lord whose word has already brought you from death to life remains the Lord who will call the dead from their graves.
