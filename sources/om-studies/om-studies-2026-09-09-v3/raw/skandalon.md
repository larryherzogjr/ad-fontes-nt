Two instructions can seem impossible to reconcile: preach Christ without compromise, and do not put a stumbling block before your neighbor. Scripture gives both. It also gives the distinction that keeps courage from becoming cruelty and love from becoming silence.

## The Word

**σκάνδαλον** (*skandalon*) means *stumbling block*, *trap*, or *offense*. It names something that brings about a fall. In the New Testament, the concern is often a person's relation to faith and obedience, not simply the experience of being annoyed.

The word does not tell us by itself whether the obstacle should be removed. We must ask what people are stumbling over and why.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 9:33 and 1 Peter 2:8 apply the image of a stumbling stone to Christ. The same Christ in whom believers trust is rejected by those who will not receive him.

Romans 14:13 commands Christians not to put an obstacle or trap before a brother or sister. Here Paul is discussing the use of freedom in matters such as food. Someone's insistence on exercising a liberty can encourage a fellow Christian to act against conscience.

These are different situations. Refusing Christ and being harmed by a Christian's loveless use of freedom must not be treated as the same problem.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul explains the center of his preaching:

> “but we preach about a crucified Christ, a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles.” (1 Corinthians 1:23, NET)

In Galatians 5:11, he says that preaching circumcision would remove the offense of the cross. The issue there is the addition of a requirement that undermines the sufficiency of Christ. Paul will not purchase acceptance at that price.

Romans 14 asks him to make a different sacrifice: the surrender of a freedom for the neighbor's good. Food is not worth destroying someone for whom Christ died. [Mōria](/greek/moria/) explores the related language of foolishness in the preaching of the cross.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Christian love cannot remove the need for repentance or the promise of forgiveness in Christ alone. The truth that saves may expose precisely what a hearer wants to defend. A promise emptied of its content cannot give the comfort of the gospel.

But we can relinquish a preference, a display of sophistication, or the insistence on having our own way. In matters God has neither commanded nor forbidden, freedom is exercised with care for the neighbor. Romans 14 does not turn every objection into a new divine command; it teaches us to care about what our conduct does to another person's faith.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Sometimes any disagreement is labeled a stumbling block, as though love required never saying anything difficult. That would make Paul's own proclamation impossible.

Sometimes the offense of the cross is used to defend a needlessly harsh manner. A person may be reacting to our contempt rather than to Christ. The distinction requires honest examination: am I being asked to abandon God's truth, or can I change my conduct without abandoning it?

## So What

Keep Christ's promise clear and your own pride under scrutiny. You can speak a difficult truth patiently, and you can give up a liberty without giving up the gospel.

The neighbor before you is someone for whom Christ died. That truth supplies both the message you must not surrender and the reason to deliver it with love.
