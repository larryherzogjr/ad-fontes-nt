A weak prayer is not a prayer left without help. Romans 8 speaks of the Spirit interceding in our weakness and the risen Christ interceding at God's right hand. The Christian's confidence rests on work he is not required to perform for himself.

## The Word

**ἐντυγχάνω** (*entynchanō*) can mean *to appeal to* someone or *to intercede*. Context tells us who makes the appeal, to whom, and on whose behalf.

The verb is not restricted to a saving intercession. Romans 11:2 uses it for Elijah's appeal against Israel, and Acts 25:24 for an appeal to Festus against Paul. The comfort in Romans 8 comes from the persons interceding and their purpose, not from the verb considered alone.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 8:27 uses *entynchanō* for the Spirit's intercession on behalf of the saints according to God's will. Verse 34 uses it for Christ's intercession for us.

Verse 26 uses the compound **ὑπερεντυγχάνω** (*hyperentynchanō*). The NET renders it *intercedes for us*. Paul says the Spirit helps precisely where we do not know how we ought to pray.

Hebrews 7:25 uses the simple verb when describing the continuing work of the living high priest. Christ's death does not end in a vacant office: he lives forever and continues to act for those who come to God through him.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul identifies Christ as the one:

> “who is at the right hand of God, and who also is interceding for us.” (Romans 8:34, NET)

The sentence answers a question about condemnation. Christ died and rose; his present intercession belongs to the same saving work. The believer is not left to answer every accusation from his own resources.

Hebrews 7:25 likewise grounds Christ's ability to save completely in his enduring priesthood. The surrounding verses affirm the sufficiency of his offering of himself once for all. Continuing intercession does not mean a repeated sacrifice is needed.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Spirit's help and the Son's intercession give prayer a foundation deeper than our concentration, vocabulary, or emotional strength. God knows the weakness that makes prayer difficult. Paul names that weakness before speaking of the help given.

Nor should Christ's intercession be pictured as a merciful Son trying to overcome an unwilling Father. Romans 8:32 has just said that God gave his own Son for us. Father, Son, and Spirit act for our salvation; they are not competing over whether to show mercy.

This promise also avoids making unusual speech or a particular inner experience the proof that the Spirit is helping. The assurance comes from what Scripture says he does, including when the believer lacks adequate words.

## So What

Pray the words you have, even if they are few. You do not have to make them impressive enough to enlist Christ's attention or activate the Spirit's concern.

When your prayer feels inadequate, return to the intercession already promised. The Savior who died and rose for you lives to intercede for you. Your weakness is the place where the Spirit helps, not a reason to believe he has withdrawn.
