Prayer can ask for help, give thanks, and carry another person's need before God. Paul gathers those actions together rather than requiring Christians to choose only one. The church's varied prayers give expression to a life that receives everything from its Father.

## The Word

**δέησις** (*deēsis*) means *petition* or *supplication*: an appeal arising from need. It is one of four prayer terms Paul places together in 1 Timothy 2:1.

The others are **προσευχή** (*proseuchē*), prayer; **ἔντευξις** (*enteuxis*), intercession or appeal; and **εὐχαριστία** (*eucharistia*), thanksgiving. These words help us notice different aspects of prayer without making four sealed compartments that never overlap.

## Range of Meaning

Philippians 4:6 joins prayer and petition with thanksgiving as believers bring their requests to God. Ephesians 6:18 calls for persevering prayer and petitions for all the saints.

Luke 1:13 gives the word a personal setting: Zechariah is told that his prayer has been heard, as the angel announces the birth of John. James 5:16 uses it when teaching Christians to pray for one another.

A petition can therefore concern one's own need or the needs of others. Its importance does not depend on whether it is spoken in a church service or in a quiet room.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul urges that:

> “requests, prayers, intercessions, and thanks be offered on behalf of all people” (1 Timothy 2:1, NET)

He expressly includes rulers, connecting prayer with the desire for a peaceful life in godliness and dignity. The passage then speaks of God's desire for all to be saved and of Christ, who gave himself as a ransom for all.

The church's prayer is thus wider than the circle of people it already likes or expects to agree with it. Neighbors, rulers, strangers, and opponents can all be brought before God.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The congregation's prayers give people words for needs they might otherwise forget. A shared petition can turn our attention toward the sick, those in authority, the church's ministry, and people enduring distress far from us.

Such prayer does not require pretending that every person or government acts well. We can ask for correction, protection, wisdom, and peace. Praying for someone is not an endorsement of everything he does.

Thanksgiving also belongs beside asking. It remembers gifts already received while acknowledging needs that remain. This is not a technique for making a request more persuasive; it is the response of people who depend on God's goodness.

## So What

You do not need to classify a prayer perfectly before speaking it. Name the need and bring it to your Father through Christ.

Let the congregation's prayers broaden your own. When an unfamiliar person or distant trouble is named, that petition can become yours as well. And when someone else prays for a need you cannot put into words, you can receive those words and join the Amen.
