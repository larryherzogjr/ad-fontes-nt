A person can speak very modestly and still rely on a private list of reasons God ought to approve of him. Another can speak boldly about Christ without claiming any merit of his own. Paul's teaching about boasting reaches deeper than volume or manners. It concerns the ground of confidence.

## The Word

**καυχάομαι** (*kauchaomai*) means *to boast* or *to glory in*. English translations sometimes use *rejoice* when the context concerns joyful confidence rather than self-promotion.

Romans 3:27 uses the related noun **καύχησις** (*kauchēsis*), *boasting*. It belongs to the same argument but should not be counted as an occurrence of the verb.

## Range of Meaning

Paul excludes boasting in works as the basis of standing before God. Yet he also tells believers to boast in the Lord. The difference is the object of confidence, not a contradiction in his advice.

Romans 5:11 speaks of rejoicing in God through Christ, through whom reconciliation has been received. Second Corinthians 12:9 speaks of boasting in weakness so that Christ's power may rest on Paul. Galatians 6:14 locates his boast in the cross. None turns human achievement into the reason for God's mercy.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul concludes his account of God's call with this instruction:

> “Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord.” (1 Corinthians 1:31, NET)

The preceding verse identifies Christ as our wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption. The instruction follows from what God gives in him. There is no need to manufacture a more impressive identity before belonging to Christ.

Ephesians 2:8–10 likewise places salvation outside the sphere of earned achievement and then gives good works their proper place. They are the life for which God has created his people in Christ, not the basis on which they may boast of obtaining salvation.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The exclusion of boasting protects the gift. If justification rests on Christ received by faith, there is no remaining contribution by which the sinner may claim a share of the credit.

That does not require denying every ability or pretending that faithful work has no value. Gifts can be acknowledged with thanks, and the neighbor can benefit from their use. The question is whether we turn them into grounds for confidence before God.

Paul's weakness also needs careful hearing. He asked for relief from his thorn; suffering was not a spiritual trophy he wanted to collect. Christ's sufficient grace enabled him to speak of weakness without making it the end of hope.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Reducing boasting to bad manners leaves its theological root untouched. A humble presentation can conceal reliance on one's decency, orthodoxy, sacrifice, or even humility itself.

But boasting in the Lord is not permission to display contempt for people who believe differently. The confession that everything is received leaves no room for treating another sinner as naturally beneath us. Confidence in Christ and patient speech belong together.

## So What

Notice where you look when you need reassurance that God receives you. If you find yourself rehearsing your record, turn again to Christ's promise.

You may speak with confidence because the cross is sufficient. [Stauros](/greek/stauros/) follows that saving work, and [Skybalon](/greek/skybalon/) follows Paul's surrender of the credentials he once treated as gain.
