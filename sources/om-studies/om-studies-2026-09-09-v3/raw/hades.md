The risen Christ says that he holds the keys of death and Hades. The confession gives comfort because death's dominion is not beyond his authority. Understanding the word helps us hear that promise without turning the Bible into a map of the unseen world.

## The Word

**ᾅδης** (*hadēs*) names Hades, the realm of the dead. It is distinct from **γέεννα** (*geenna*), the word translated *hell* in passages such as Matthew 10:28 concerning final judgment.

English translations have sometimes used *hell* for more than one Greek term. The distinction matters, but it does not make every occurrence of *hadēs* a description of a neutral or painless condition. Luke 16:23 explicitly speaks of torment there.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew 16:18 speaks of the gates of Hades in Christ's promise concerning his church. Acts 2:27–31 uses the word while interpreting the psalm's promise that God's Holy One would not be abandoned to death and decay.

Revelation 20:13–14 distinguishes Death and Hades from the lake of fire: they surrender their dead and are themselves thrown into it. We should preserve the text's distinctions rather than flatten every image into one English label.

## Where You’ll Meet It

The risen Jesus declares:

> “I hold the keys of death and of Hades!” (Revelation 1:18, NET)

He identifies himself as the one who was dead and now lives forever. The authority belongs to the crucified and risen Lord, not to an impersonal force that competes with him.

Acts 2 likewise directs attention to Christ's resurrection. Peter explains that David's words concern the Messiah, whose body did not undergo decay. The passage proclaims what God has done; it does not answer every question about the condition of all the departed.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration IX.1–3, distinguishes Christ's burial from his descent. It confesses that the whole person, God and man, descended after burial, conquered the devil, and destroyed hell's power. It then warns against speculation about the manner of this mystery.

The descent is confessed as victory, not as an additional payment of suffering needed to complete the cross. Its comfort is that Christ has defeated the powers that threaten those who belong to him.

This doctrine does not follow simply from choosing a dictionary gloss for *hadēs*. Nor should the descent be reduced to another name for burial. The Formula explicitly distinguishes them and grounds confidence in Christ's victory.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Collapsing Hades and Gehenna can obscure the passages' different images. But a sharp distinction must not become the claim that Hades can never involve torment; Luke prevents that conclusion.

Likewise, the creed's descent should not be explained as Christ continuing to suffer punishment after his saving death. The confessional account directs us to his triumph and asks us to stop where the Word stops.

## So What

The Lord who holds death's keys has not left your future in an enemy's hands. Receive the forgiveness and life he promises, even when death's power feels overwhelming.
