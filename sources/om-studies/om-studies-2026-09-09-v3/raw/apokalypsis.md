The last day will reveal the Lord Christians already trust, yet it will also bring the resurrection and judgment for which they wait. Revelation means more than catastrophe and more than discovering that nothing needed to change. God will make his promised future manifest.

## The Word

**ἀποκάλυψις** (*apokalypsis*) means *revelation*, *disclosure*, or *unveiling*. It names a making-known of what was hidden or not yet seen.

The English word *apocalypse* often suggests overwhelming disaster. That association must not be substituted for the Greek noun's meaning. A passage may include judgment, but the noun itself names revelation.

## Range of Meaning

First Corinthians 1:7 describes believers waiting for the revelation of Jesus Christ. First Peter 1:7 and 1:13 also connects their tested faith and future hope with his revealing.

Romans 8:19 uses the noun for the revelation of God's children. Creation waits for the public manifestation of the people whose promised glory is not yet apparent amid suffering and decay.

Revelation 1:1 opens with the revelation of Jesus Christ. The following clauses describe its being given and communicated to his servants. The opening keeps Christ central while introducing what God makes known through the book.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul describes believers as those who:

> “wait for the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ.” (1 Corinthians 1:7, NET)

He immediately adds the promise that Christ will strengthen them to the end and points to God's faithfulness. Their waiting rests on the one who has called them, not on their ability to decipher a timetable.

Second Thessalonians 1:7–10 places the revelation of Jesus within both relief for the afflicted and judgment. It cannot be reduced to a private moment of insight or to reassurance that evil has no lasting consequences.

First Peter directs hope toward the grace to be brought when Jesus is revealed. Expectation shapes sober, faithful life in the present.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Christ's lordship is true now, even where the church appears weak and believers suffer. The future unveiling will not make him Lord for the first time. It will manifest the glory that faith presently knows through his Word.

Yet the last day is not merely a change in what observers understand. The dead will be raised, judgment will occur, and creation's bondage will end. The promised future includes God's acts as well as their public disclosure.

That gives waiting a definite content. Christians continue hearing the gospel, receiving Christ's gifts, and serving neighbors. They need not escape their callings or turn every disturbance into a coded announcement of the date.

## So What

Let the promise of Christ's appearing steady the work you have to do now. What is hidden from sight is not therefore absent from God's care.

When the church's weakness troubles you, return to the Lord's promise rather than demanding an immediate display of glory. The Savior who is already yours will be revealed, and his faithfulness is the ground of your waiting.
