A gift can become a purchase in disguise if the giver quietly attaches a price. Paul excludes that arrangement when he describes justification as freely given. Redemption has a cost, but its cost is not collected from the sinner who receives it.

## The Word

**δωρεάν** (*dōrean*) means *freely*, *as a gift*, or *without cause*. It is an adverb: it tells us something about how an action happens.

Romans 3:24 uses it for being justified freely. John 15:25 uses the same word for hatred without a reason. The different settings show why a gloss must be applied with attention to what each sentence is saying.

## Range of Meaning

In 2 Thessalonians 3:8, Paul says that he did not eat others' food without paying. Here the adverb concerns ordinary financial support. Revelation 21:6 and 22:17 use it for the water of life given free of charge.

John's use is different. Jesus has given his opponents no just cause for their hatred. In Romans, sinners have supplied no merit that makes justification a debt God owes. The shared word does not make these two actions morally alike; it exposes the absence of a deserved basis in their different contexts.

## Where You’ll Meet It

After declaring that all have sinned, Paul writes:

> “But they are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.” (Romans 3:24, NET)

The sentence identifies both the manner of the gift and its basis in Christ's redemption. *Freely* does not mean that sin was inexpensive to deal with or that God has no reason for showing mercy. It means the gift is not bought by the recipient's deserving.

Revelation's invitation preserves the same generosity. The thirsty are invited to take the water of life without payment (22:17). Their thirst names their need; it is not a currency that buys what God gives.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Grace excludes merit as the basis of justification. Christ's saving work supplies the ground of our confidence. Faith receives that work and its promise rather than providing a final payment that makes them effective.

This is why the distinction between receiving and earning matters. Scripture really calls people to believe, repent, and come to Christ. Those calls are not evidence that salvation has ceased to be free. Through the gospel the Spirit creates the faith that receives God's gift.

[Epangelia](/greek/epangelia/) considers the promise faith hears. [Logizomai](/greek/logizomai/) follows the crediting of righteousness apart from works. Neither permits the conscience to turn faith itself into a superior kind of achievement.

## Where People Get It Wrong

A sermon can announce free forgiveness and then leave the hearer searching for a sufficient desire, decision, or degree of perseverance to qualify for it. The question has shifted from Christ's promise to the quality of our response.

The answer is not to make faith unnecessary. Unbelief rejects the gift. The answer is to keep faith in its receiving place and to locate the reason for God's favorable verdict in Christ, not in an admirable act of acceptance.

## So What

You may bring need rather than payment. You do not have to discover something commendable in yourself before asking for what Christ has secured.

Receive the forgiveness that is given freely, and let gratitude take shape in service to the neighbor. Such service is the fruit of the gift, not its hidden bill.
