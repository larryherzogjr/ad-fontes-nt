Jesus does not describe coming to him as a spiritual accomplishment we can produce independently. He speaks of the Father's drawing. That statement humbles human confidence while giving faith a source outside our unstable resolve.

## The Word

**ἕλκω** (*helkō*) means *to draw* or *drag*. Some lexical resources also list the extended present form *helkyō*. The verb can describe physical pulling and can be used figuratively of drawing people.

John 6:44 uses the form *helkysē*. Identifying that form matters for finding the word, but the physical force of a fishing net being hauled cannot simply be transferred into every statement about conversion.

## Range of Meaning

John 21 uses the verb for drawing a net containing fish. John 18:10 uses it for Peter drawing a sword. John 12:32 uses it in Jesus' statement about drawing all people to himself when he is lifted up.

The surrounding sentence in John 12 identifies the kind of death Jesus would die. John 6 places the Father's drawing beside hearing and learning from him. These are theological contexts, not descriptions of people being moved like unthinking objects.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus says:

> “No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him” (John 6:44, NET)

He then promises resurrection on the last day. The next verse connects coming to him with hearing and learning from the Father. The gift of life and the Father's teaching belong within the explanation.

The opening denial of human ability should not be softened into a claim that we merely need a little encouragement to complete an otherwise independent decision. Jesus locates the initiative in his Father.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Small Catechism's Third Article explanation confesses that we cannot believe in Jesus Christ or come to him by our own reason or strength. The Holy Spirit calls through the gospel. Augsburg Confession XVIII likewise distinguishes ordinary human choices from the spiritual righteousness that requires the Spirit's work.

This is the confession of God's sole saving initiative in conversion. It does not mean that a regenerated Christian never wills, acts, or serves. Nor does it establish that every working of grace is irresistible. The Lutheran confession also takes seriously the biblical warnings against resisting God's Word.

The verb therefore serves a larger scriptural account. We do not need to weaken it to preserve human self-salvation, and we do not need to force it into a proof that every person drawn is mechanically compelled.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Arguments sometimes turn *draw* into a choice between a harmless invitation and physical coercion. John's context is richer than either picture. The Father truly acts; people hear and learn; Christ promises resurrection.

John 12:32 should also be heard without treating the breadth of Christ's drawing as proof that all people finally believe. The reach of the gospel and the reality of unbelief both belong to the biblical witness.

## So What

Your inability to manufacture faith is not a reason to avoid the gospel. It is a reason to hear the Word through which the Spirit works.

Ask God for faith and help in unbelief. Then listen to the Savior who gives himself to sinners, rather than searching for a hidden spiritual capacity that will make you worthy to come.
