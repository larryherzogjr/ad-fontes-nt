Sometimes the Christian life sounds like getting dressed for a role you have not yet learned to play. Put on patience. Put on kindness. Put on Christ. You try, fail, and wonder whether the garment ever really belonged to you.

Paul does command Christians to put on Christ. But he also tells baptized Christians that they already have. Reading both sentences keeps the command from swallowing the gift.

## The Word

**ἐνδύω** (*endyō*) means *to put on clothing* or *to clothe*. Greek uses forms of the verb for dressing someone and for putting something on oneself. The clothing image is ordinary enough: what is put on covers the person and marks how that person appears.

In Galatians 3:27, *enedysasthe* is an aorist middle indicative. The indicative states something; it does not issue a command. The middle form suits the act of putting on clothing. Paul says what the baptized have done in being baptized into Christ.

## Range of Meaning

The image can name Christ himself, the new person, or the conduct fitting that new life. Those uses belong together without becoming interchangeable.

To be clothed with Christ is to receive an identity and standing that come from him. To clothe oneself with mercy or patience is to live toward others in keeping with that gift. The same verb serves both statements; the sentences tell us whether Paul is announcing what has happened or directing how Christians should live.

## Where You’ll Meet It

> “For all of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ.” (Galatians 3:27, NET)

The previous verse speaks of being God's children through faith in Christ. Baptism and faith belong together in Paul's account. Baptism is not presented as the graduation ceremony of people who have made themselves sufficiently like Jesus.

Romans 13:14 then commands Christians to put on the Lord Jesus Christ and stop providing for sinful desires. Colossians 3:10 describes their new clothing before verse 12 calls them to clothe themselves with mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. Ephesians 4:24 likewise places putting on the new person within instruction about the Christian life.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Here is a useful picture of righteousness received from another. Christ is our righteousness; our standing before God does not grow out of successful self-improvement. The garment belongs to Christ before it clothes us.

That picture must retain Paul's connection to [Baptism](/greek/baptizo/). He has not offered a general invitation to imagine ourselves wearing Christ. He speaks to people baptized into him and identifies the gift attached to that act.

The commands that follow are therefore serious without becoming the price of adoption. Because you belong to Christ, the old ways of lying, cruelty, and indulgence no longer fit. God calls you to real renewal in the life he has given.

## Where People Get It Wrong

If every occurrence becomes a command, Galatians 3:27 loses its comfort. You hear only an assignment: become Christlike enough to count as clothed. Paul's announcement gets moved to the end of a process he never describes.

The opposite error makes the announcement cancel the commands. But Romans and Colossians address Christians who really must resist sin and practice mercy. Received righteousness does not make an unforgiving life harmless.

The order keeps both truths in view. The gift establishes the person who is commanded. The command directs that person toward the neighbor who needs love.

## So What

When you confess the day's sins, do not turn Baptism into a garment you must earn back by tomorrow evening. Return in repentance and faith to Christ's promise.

Then put on patience with the person who tries yours. Speak truth where you were tempted to lie. The Lord has clothed you with himself. The life he now calls you to live has somewhere firm to begin.
