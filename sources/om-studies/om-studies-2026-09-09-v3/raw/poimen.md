A congregation can ask its pastor to do many things. Some are useful; others can fill every available hour while leaving the central work undone. The New Testament's word for pastor gives the office a shape: a shepherd cares for a flock that belongs to Christ.

## The Word

**ποιμήν** (*poimēn*) means *shepherd*. In Ephesians 4:11, the NET translates its plural form as *pastors*. The familiar church title carries the image of tending sheep.

That image includes feeding, guiding, protecting, and attending to the vulnerable. It describes responsibility for those entrusted to one's care. It gives no permission to treat people as possessions or to demand unquestioning loyalty to a personality.

## Range of Meaning

Jesus identifies himself as the good shepherd in John 10. Hebrews 13:20 calls him the great shepherd of the sheep, raised from the dead through the blood of the eternal covenant.

First Peter 5:2 uses the related verb **ποιμαίνω** (*poimainō*), calling elders to give shepherding care to God's flock. Verse 4 calls Christ the Chief Shepherd, using the compound **ἀρχιποίμην** (*archipoimēn*). The ministry's relationship to Christ is visible even in this vocabulary: the flock remains his.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus says:

> “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” (John 10:11, NET)

The contrast is with the hired hand who abandons the sheep when danger arrives. Christ knows his sheep, gives his life for them, and brings them together under his voice.

Ephesians 4:11–16 names pastors and teachers among Christ's gifts to his church. Their work serves the body's maturity, stability in the truth, and growth in love. The goal is a people who know Christ and are no longer carried away by deceptive teaching.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Augsburg Confession V identifies the ministry with the gospel and sacraments through which God gives faith. Article XIV teaches that public preaching, teaching, and sacramental administration require a proper call. Together they keep the office attached to Christ's gifts and the church's ordered life.

A pastor therefore feeds the flock by preaching the Word and administering the sacraments. He teaches, visits, warns, consoles, and prays in service of that charge. These are ways of caring for people whom Christ bought with his blood.

First Peter also forbids domineering leadership and shameful gain. Being called to shepherd does not place a minister beyond correction. It makes him answerable for how he treats the flock.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Planning, organization, and responsible leadership matter. The mistake is making numerical growth, constant activity, or the pastor's energy the measure of his calling.

A congregation may grow under faithful care or endure a difficult season under faithful care. Numbers alone cannot tell the difference between true teaching and false teaching, patient service and neglect. Equally, a claim of faithfulness cannot excuse laziness or mistreatment. The question is whether the shepherd is carrying out the work entrusted to him.

## So What

Ask whether your pastor is giving you Christ's Word and gifts, and make room for that work. A quiet visit to someone dying may matter more than an impressive new program.

Your pastor also needs the Shepherd he proclaims. Christ's care reaches the whole flock, including the man called to tend it. Pray for him, receive faithful teaching, and keep the Lord's voice at the center.
