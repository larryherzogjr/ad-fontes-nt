Talk about curses can make an anxious person search endlessly for an unknown spiritual cause behind each misfortune. Paul brings a more definite accusation into view. God's law requires obedience, and sinners have not given it. He then announces an equally definite deliverance: Christ became a curse for us.

## The Word

**κατάρα** (*katara*) means *curse*. In Galatians 3, it names the condemnation pronounced by the law against those who do not keep it.

This is not an impersonal force that can be manipulated with the right technique. Paul is speaking about our standing before God and the redemption Christ accomplishes on our behalf.

## Range of Meaning

The wider biblical language of cursing has several settings. We should not pretend that every occurrence refers to the same situation or that Scripture discusses only one kind of curse.

Galatians 3 makes its subject clear. Verse 10 places those who rely on works of the law under a curse because the law demands continuing obedience to everything it commands. Verse 13 announces Christ's redemption from that curse. Verse 14 speaks of the blessing of Abraham and the promised Spirit received through faith.

The contrast is between the law's condemnation and God's promised blessing, not between an unlucky life and a newly guaranteed run of success.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul writes:

> “Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us. For it is written: ‘Cursed is everyone who is hung on a tree.’” (Galatians 3:13, BSB)

He supports the statement by citing the law about someone hung on a tree. In Deuteronomy 21:22–23, an executed person's exposed body is to be buried the same day. Paul applies that language to Christ's death.

The words *for us* carry the saving exchange. The sinless Christ takes the condemned place that belongs to sinners. The blessing that follows is received by faith, as Paul makes explicit in verse 14. The cross is not merely a display of how badly human authorities can treat an innocent person.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The law's accusation cannot be answered by locating someone else who sinned first or by improving the spiritual atmosphere around us. We need the Redeemer who answers for our sin before God.

Christ's becoming a curse does not make him personally sinful. He bears the judgment for sinners. The gospel announces his completed work rather than asking us to finish the removal of condemnation through a special prayer or achievement.

Nor does this promise mean that Christians cease to suffer the consequences of others' wrongdoing or their own. Forgiveness and deliverance from condemnation are not a guarantee of an undamaged family history. They give us a secure place from which to face that history truthfully.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Some curse-breaking teaching seeks to address real fears about repeated family sins and destructive patterns. Those concerns deserve patient care. The mistake is treating a believer's freedom before God as something Christ has left dependent on discovering a hidden curse or completing the correct ritual.

Repentance, prayer, practical help, and resistance to evil all have their place. They receive and live from Christ's victory; they do not add an omitted piece to it.

## So What

Bring the actual sin and the actual fear to Christ. You do not need to know every secret of your ancestry before hearing his promise of forgiveness.

The law's curse has met its answer in the crucified Redeemer. [Stauros](/greek/stauros/) directs you to that cross, where Christ gave himself for you.
