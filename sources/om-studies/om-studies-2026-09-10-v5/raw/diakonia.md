The New Testament uses service language for both material help and the ministry of the Word. The common vocabulary honors both without making every task identical. What is being given and who has been entrusted with giving it still matter.

## The Word

**διακονία** (*diakonia*) means *service* or *ministry*. It is related to [Diakonos](/greek/diakonos/), servant or minister. The noun can name a task, a sphere of service, or the ministry entrusted to someone.

The word does not automatically designate an ordained office whenever it appears. Neither does its broad range prove that the public ministry has no distinctive commission. The particular passage must establish which service is in view.

## Range of Meaning

Acts 6 uses *diakonia* for the daily distribution and for the ministry of the Word. The apostles' need to give attention to prayer and the Word does not make the neglected widows unimportant. The congregation arranges care precisely because their need is serious.

Second Corinthians 9 uses the word in connection with the collection for the saints. Second Corinthians 5 names the ministry of reconciliation. Material provision and proclamation can both be service while differing in what they deliver.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul speaks of God:

> “All this is from God, who reconciled us to Himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation:” (2 Corinthians 5:18, BSB)

The preceding clause identifies God as the one who reconciled us to himself through Christ. The following verses describe the entrusted message and the appeal to be reconciled to God. The ministers serve a reconciliation whose source is already God's action.

They do not negotiate a compromise between equally valid accounts of sin. Nor do they create reconciliation through their diplomatic skill. They announce God's work in Christ and address its appeal to their hearers.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Augsburg Confession V identifies the office of preaching and the sacraments as God's appointed means for giving faith through the Spirit. The minister's service is therefore defined by a gift he has received to distribute.

The broader service of Christians remains indispensable. Feeding someone in need is a real work of love; it does not need to become a sermon to count. Preaching forgiveness is also a real service; it should not be replaced by material aid on the assumption that people have only bodily needs.

Holding the tasks together without confusing them helps a congregation care well. Different responsibilities can be recognized without creating a contest over whose service is more impressive. The same Lord gives gifts for the good of his people and their neighbors.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Calling every activity a ministry can obscure the specific responsibilities of those called to preach and administer the sacraments. Restricting every meaningful service to the pastor can leave the congregation's many gifts unused.

Acts 6 presents a more concrete question: who needs care, and how can the necessary work be faithfully carried out? It does not ask every task to acquire the same title.

## So What

Support the ministry that brings you Christ's Word and gifts. Also notice the material needs that can be met through your own time, skill, or resources.

The person helped need not admire the work for it to matter. [Leitourgos](/greek/leitourgos/) explores another service word whose uses reach into ordinary public responsibilities.
