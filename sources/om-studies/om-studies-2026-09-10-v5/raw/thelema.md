Praying for God's will to be done can sound like surrender to whatever happens next. The Lord's Prayer teaches something more definite. We ask our Father to carry out his good and gracious will among us, overcoming what opposes his Word and keeping us in faith.

## The Word

**θέλημα** (*thelēma*) means *will*: what someone wants, purposes, or desires. It occurs in the Lord's Prayer and in Jesus' prayer in Gethsemane.

The word itself does not answer every question about providence. We learn what God's will means for us by listening to the passages in which he makes it known.

## Range of Meaning

Ephesians 1:9 speaks of God revealing the mystery of his will in Christ. First Thessalonians 4:3 identifies God's will for believers as their sanctification, specifically including abstaining from sexual immorality. His revealed will concerns both our salvation and the holy life that follows.

First Timothy 2:4 says that God wants all people to be saved and come to know the truth. That verse uses the related verb **θέλω** (*thelō*), not the noun *thelēma*. Its place in the discussion comes from what it teaches about God's saving desire, not from an identical Greek form.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus teaches us to pray:

> “Your kingdom come, Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.” (Matthew 6:10, BSB)

We make this petition to our Father. It belongs beside the prayers for his name to be honored and his kingdom to come. Its setting gives the request a direction that resignation to anonymous fate could never supply.

In Matthew 26:42, Jesus again prays that the Father's will be done as he faces the cup he must drink. This obedience is costly. His anguish does not make his prayer faithless, and his prayer does not make the anguish unreal.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Small Catechism's Third Petition explains that God's good and gracious will comes about without our prayer, but we ask that it also come about among us. God breaks the opposition of the devil, the world, and our sinful flesh and keeps us steadfast in his Word and faith until life's end.

That explanation identifies something concrete to ask for. Preserve me in your promise. Hinder what draws me away from you. Bring your saving purpose to its fulfillment among us.

It also prevents an appeal to God's hidden will from canceling his revealed mercy. We do not know why every particular suffering is permitted. We do know the Savior whom God has given and the invitation to pray for all people.

Nor does submission to providence make obedience unnecessary. God's commands give us work to do while outcomes remain uncertain: protect the vulnerable, fulfill our responsibilities, and resist the sins he forbids.

## So What

You can ask for relief from suffering without knowing how your Father will answer that particular request. Jesus' prayer gives you permission to name the burden honestly and entrust yourself to him.

You can also take the next faithful step without discovering a hidden plan for every decision. Attend to God's Word and your neighbor's need. The Third Petition asks your Father to sustain precisely that life of faith and obedience.
