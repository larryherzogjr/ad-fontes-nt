A delay in judgment can be mistaken for indifference. Scripture teaches us to recognize something else in God's waiting: patience directed toward repentance. The time before Christ's return is time in which his saving promise is still being proclaimed.

## The Word

**μακροθυμία** (*makrothymia*) means *patience* or *longsuffering*. The picture of being long-tempered, rather than short-tempered, helps convey the restraint involved.

That image is a starting point, not a complete definition of God's character. The passages themselves tell us why God is patient and how his patience differs from approval of evil.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 2:4 places patience alongside God's kindness and forbearance. The next verse warns against stubborn refusal to repent. Patience gives no reason to assume that judgment has been canceled.

Romans 9:22 also speaks of God's great patience in connection with judgment. First Timothy 1:16 gives the word a personal setting: Christ's patience toward Paul becomes an example for those who will believe in him for eternal life.

Galatians 5:22 names patience among the fruit of the Spirit. What believers receive from God also begins to shape how they live with other people.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Peter writes:

> “Consider also that our Lord’s patience brings salvation, just as our beloved brother Paul also wrote you with the wisdom God gave him.” (2 Peter 3:15, BSB)

Earlier he explains that the Lord is patient, desiring repentance rather than destruction (3:9). That verse uses the related verb **μακροθυμέω** (*makrothymeō*); verse 15 uses the noun.

The chapter answers those who interpret the apparent delay of Christ's coming as evidence against his promise. Peter affirms both patience and the coming day of the Lord. Waiting should lead believers into holiness and hope, not complacency.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

God's patience is gracious without making his warnings empty. The law names what must be repented of; the gospel gives the Savior to whom sinners may turn. More time is not an invitation to postpone receiving the gift.

Paul's example is especially helpful to someone who thinks a history of opposition places him beyond mercy. Christ's patience toward a persecutor demonstrates that the gospel reaches people who have no record of deserving it.

The Spirit's fruit then gives patience a place in ordinary vocation. Parents, pastors, coworkers, and neighbors encounter people who do not change at the pace they would prefer. Patience can listen, teach again, and make room for repentance without declaring wrongdoing acceptable.

It also need not leave someone unprotected from harm. Patient love can set limits and intervene. The question is whether our action seeks the neighbor's good, rather than merely satisfying our irritation.

## So What

Receive the present opportunity to repent and hear Christ's promise. God's patience is a reason to come to him, not a reason to keep him waiting.

Then consider where you can extend patient care to someone else. You do not have to control the timing of another person's growth. You can speak truth, offer help, and remember how much patience you have received from the Lord.
