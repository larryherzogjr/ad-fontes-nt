The ascension does not turn Jesus into a memory of someone who used to be present. The risen Lord is taken up into heaven and reigns at God's right hand. The same Jesus remains the church's Savior and will return as he has promised.

## The Word

**ἀναλαμβάνω** (*analambanō*) means *to take up* or *to receive up*. In the ascension passages it appears in forms describing Jesus being taken up.

The verb names what happens; it does not by itself provide a map of heaven or a complete account of Christ's present mode of existence. Those questions must be answered from Scripture's wider witness.

## Range of Meaning

Acts 1:2 and 1:22 use the verb to mark the end of Jesus' visible earthly association with the apostles before the ascension. First Timothy 3:16 includes his being taken up in glory within its confession of his work.

Mark 16:19 also uses the verb. That verse belongs to Mark's longer ending, whose textual history is disputed. The doctrine of the ascension does not depend on that passage alone; Acts gives an independent and explicit account.

## Where You’ll Meet It

At the ascension, the apostles hear of:

> “‘Men of Galilee,’ they said, ‘why do you stand here looking into the sky? This same Jesus, who has been taken from you into heaven, will come back in the same way you have seen Him go into heaven.’” (Acts 1:11, BSB)

The messengers promise his return. The preceding scene describes Jesus being lifted up and hidden from their sight by a cloud. His departure is real, and so is the distinction between the apostles' former sight of him and the life of witness now beginning.

Yet disappearance from sight is not the same as surrendering his care for the church. The Lord who sends the apostles also promises the Spirit for their mission.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration VIII.27–29, connects the Lord's Supper with the confession of the one Christ, true God and true man. His exaltation does not strip away his humanity, and his humanity is not detached from the divine majesty of the person to whom it belongs.

The right hand of God names divine rule and power, not a bounded seat that prevents Christ from fulfilling his promise to give his body and blood. The Formula therefore refuses to let a calculation about ordinary bodily location overturn his words of institution.

This does not make his humanity unreal or turn his body into a fluid spread throughout space. The confession preserves the distinction of the natures and the unity of the person. How he gives what he promises exceeds our ability to explain it by ordinary physical comparisons.

The decisive ground for receiving the Supper remains Christ's institution. We do not infer a sacrament wherever we choose from a general claim about his majesty.

## So What

The ascended Christ has not become less able to keep his promises. You can receive his Word and gifts without imagining that heaven has placed him beyond reach.

You can also wait for his return without staring into the sky for a hidden schedule. The apostles were sent into a life of witness. The reigning Lord still gives his church work to do and the promise on which that work depends.
