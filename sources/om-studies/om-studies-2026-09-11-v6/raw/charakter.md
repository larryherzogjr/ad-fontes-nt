A coin carries an impression made by a die. The impression has a definite shape: the features cut into the die appear in the metal it strikes. Hebrews takes a word from that world and uses it to speak about the Son.

## The Word

**χαρακτήρ** (*charaktēr*) is a masculine noun. In Greek usage it can name an engraving tool or a stamp, and the impression or distinctive mark produced. Hebrews uses the word for the Son's relation to God's being.

The image helps us hear the precision of the claim. An impression bears the features of what impressed it. Applied to the Son, the word speaks of the exact expression of the Father's being.

## Range of Meaning

The word's movement from a physical mark to a distinguishing feature makes it useful beyond metalworking. In Hebrews, the physical picture serves a claim about who the Son is. The author does not ask us to imagine God as metal or to reconstruct the process by which a divine Son might be manufactured.

Read the image within the sentence. The Son reveals the Father while remaining the Son; the language holds their relation before us.

## Where You’ll Meet It

The Son is called “the exact representation of His nature” (Hebrews 1:3, BSB). *Representation* renders *charaktēr*; *nature* renders [hypostasis](/greek/hypostasis/).

The surrounding words give the image its weight. Through this Son God made the world (1:2). The Son sustains everything, accomplishes cleansing for sins, and sits at God's right hand (1:3). The one who makes the Father known is also the one through whom the world exists and sinners are cleansed.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

We hear this passage within the church's confession of the Son's true deity and his distinction from the Father. The Son shares the divine being he reveals. The stamp image contributes to that confession without supplying a complete doctrine of the Trinity by itself. Its force comes within Hebrews' account of the Son's identity and work.

That is why the turn to cleansing for sins matters. The writer has not left lofty theology behind to mention something practical. The Son whose identity he has been describing is the one who has dealt with our sins.

## So What

When you listen to Christ, you are hearing the one who truly makes the Father known. You do not have to guess whether his mercy reflects the Father's disposition toward you. Hebrews joins the revelation of God and the cleansing of sinners in this one Son.
