The promise that truth will set us free is often detached from the conversation in which Jesus speaks it. In John 8, freedom belongs with continuing in his word and with the Son's deliverance from slavery to sin. The promise has a definite speaker and a definite gift.

## The Word

**ἀλήθεια** (*alētheia*) means *truth*. It is not a technical word that always means a person rather than a statement. John's Gospel, however, closely joins truth with Jesus and with the word through which he makes the Father known.

John 14:6 identifies Jesus himself as the truth. John 17:17 names God's word as truth. These statements belong together rather than requiring a choice between Christ and what he teaches.

## Range of Meaning

In John 8:31–36, truth and freedom stand within Jesus' teaching about discipleship and slavery to sin. In Galatians 2:5 and 2:14, Paul speaks of the truth of the gospel, which can be threatened by false requirements and contradicted by conduct.

First Timothy 3:15 calls the church the support and bulwark of the truth. The church is entrusted with receiving, confessing, and teaching what God has given. The image does not make truth whatever the church finds convenient to say.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus prays:

> “Sanctify them by the truth; Your word is truth.” (John 17:17, BSB)

His prayer does not oppose sanctification to teaching. The Father's word belongs to the life for which Jesus sets his people apart.

John 8 likewise connects knowing the truth with continuing in Jesus' teaching. The discussion then identifies sin as slavery and the Son as the one who sets people free. Quoting the promise as a guarantee that any interesting discovery will liberate us misses that setting.

In Galatians 2, the issue reaches the dinner table. Peter's withdrawal from eating with Gentile believers contradicts the gospel's truth. Doctrine is implicated in how people are received and treated, not merely in the accuracy of a formula they recite.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Christ gives himself through a word that can be heard, believed, and confessed. Faithful doctrine serves this gift by telling the truth about who he is and what he has done for sinners.

That does not make theological skill a substitute for faith or love. Someone may repeat a correct sentence while acting against its meaning. Paul's confrontation with Peter shows why the truth must shape both proclamation and common life.

Nor does continuing in Christ's word become a payment that buys freedom. The Son gives the freedom. His word is where disciples remain to receive what he promises, rather than a ladder by which they earn his favor.

## Where People Get It Wrong

One error seeks a warmer Jesus behind his allegedly cold teaching. Another makes correct teaching a private possession to display while disregarding the neighbor. The first separates Christ from his word; the second fails to live according to the truth it claims to defend.

Christian discernment and Christian love belong together. Both are answerable to the Lord who speaks.

## So What

Stay with Christ's word when attractive slogans ask you to leave some of it behind. Ask also whether your conduct makes room for the people whom that gospel receives.

Truth is given for confession and for consolation. Speak it clearly enough that another sinner can hear Christ's promise, and humbly enough to remember that you live by the same mercy.
