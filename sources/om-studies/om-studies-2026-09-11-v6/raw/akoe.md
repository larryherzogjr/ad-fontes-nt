Faith does not have to invent the message it trusts. Someone announces what Christ has done, and the promise reaches a person who needs it. Paul follows that movement backward in Romans 10: believing, hearing, preaching, and the sending of a messenger.

## The Word

**ἀκοή** (*akoē*) can mean *hearing*, a *report*, or *the thing heard*. The word can draw attention to receiving a message or to the message itself.

Romans 10:16–17 uses both sides of that range. Paul quotes Isaiah's question about who has believed the report, then explains that faith comes through what is heard. The report has content: the word of Christ.

## Range of Meaning

In Galatians 3:2 and 3:5, Paul contrasts works of the law with believing what the Galatians heard. They received the Spirit through the gospel of the crucified Christ. They did not first fulfill the law and then qualify for the Spirit's arrival.

First Thessalonians 2:13 describes the message heard from the apostles as God's message, active among believers. Its delivery through human speakers does not make it merely their opinion.

Hebrews 4:2 also warns that the message heard did not benefit those who failed to receive it in faith. Hearing is not a mechanical guarantee that everyone within earshot will be saved. God's effective Word can be resisted.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul writes:

> “Consequently, faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ.” (Romans 10:17, BSB)

The preceding questions show why preachers are sent. People need to hear the one upon whom they are to call. [Apostellō](/greek/apostello/) considers that sending; *akoē* directs attention to the message's arrival.

Faith is therefore dependent on a gift that comes from beyond the believer's own thoughts. You do not supply the promise by examining yourself more closely. Christ's gospel addresses you.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Augsburg Confession V teaches that God instituted the preaching office, giving the gospel and sacraments, so that we might obtain faith. Through these means the Holy Spirit works faith where and when God wills in those who hear the gospel.

The church consequently has something definite to deliver. Encouragement, historical information, and moral instruction have their places, but they cannot replace the announcement of Christ's saving work for sinners. The Spirit creates and sustains faith through that promise.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Private Bible reading is a good gift, and God works through his written Word. Paul's emphasis on hearing does not exclude readers, people who are deaf, or the many ways Scripture can be communicated. The external Word is external because it comes from God, not because sound waves are indispensable.

The error is making a devotional achievement the source of faith, or treating the gospel as neutral information upon which an independently generated faith later acts. Reading and listening receive a Word that is already God's instrument; neither earns its power.

## So What

When faith feels weak, you need not manufacture a stronger inward condition before returning to the promise. Hear it again. Read the Scriptures. Receive the preaching and sacramental gifts Christ has provided through his church.

Their purpose is not to congratulate you for having faith already. God gives his Word to bring you to trust Christ and to keep you in that trust.
