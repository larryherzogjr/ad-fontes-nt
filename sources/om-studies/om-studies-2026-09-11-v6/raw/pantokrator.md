The creed places *Almighty* beside *Father*. The one who rules all things is the one Christians confess as their Father. His power is not a possibility waiting to become useful. Creation already depends on him, and the church already lives under his care.

## The Word

**παντοκράτωρ** (*pantokratōr*) means *Almighty* or *ruler of all*. The BSB often renders it *All-Powerful*. It names God's sovereign power, not merely his ability to accomplish an unusually difficult task.

The word's parts point toward all and ruling or power. They do not, by themselves, supply an entire doctrine of providence. Scripture tells us who this ruler is and how he deals with his creatures.

## Range of Meaning

In Revelation, the title belongs to the Lord whose rule is not overturned by the world's violence. It appears in worship, judgment, and the vision of the new Jerusalem. Almighty power is neither morally indifferent nor detached from history.

Second Corinthians 6:18 places the title within a promise of fatherly relationship. The Lord who receives his people as sons and daughters is the Almighty. His authority and his care belong together.

## Where You’ll Meet It

The heavenly multitude praises God:

> “And I heard a sound like the roar of a great multitude, like the rushing of many waters, and like a mighty rumbling of thunder, crying out: ‘Hallelujah! For the Lord our God the Almighty reigns.’” (Revelation 19:6, BSB)

Revelation 1:8 joins the title to the Lord who is, who was, and who is coming. In 4:8, the living creatures worship him as holy. Their praise leads into the acknowledgment that all things exist because he created them.

At the end of the book, the Lord God the All-Powerful and the Lamb are the city's temple (21:22). The culmination is God's presence with his people. The power confessed at the beginning of the creed is not left behind when Scripture speaks of salvation.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Small Catechism explains the First Article through the gifts that sustain ordinary life: body and reason, food and clothing, home and family, protection and preservation. These come from God's fatherly goodness and mercy, without merit on our part.

That explanation brings divine power close enough to give thanks for at the table. God preserves creation through the ordinary means by which life is supported. A farmer's work and a neighbor's help need not compete with his care; they can serve it.

This does not promise that Christians will escape deprivation, illness, or death. Revelation itself addresses people who suffer under hostile powers. They confess God's rule while those powers still wound them, trusting the Lord whose victory is revealed in the Lamb.

## So What

You can ask your Father for what your body needs. Such prayer is not beneath a God occupied with larger matters. The First Article teaches you to receive daily provision as his gift and to answer with thanks, service, and obedience.

When provision comes through your hands to someone else, it remains his gift. Almighty care makes room for your vocation. It gives you a reason to serve without imagining that the whole world rests on your shoulders.
