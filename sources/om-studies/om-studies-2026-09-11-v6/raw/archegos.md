Hebrews tells weary Christians to keep their eyes on Jesus. That can sound like advice to imitate a successful runner: study his technique, then produce the same result. But the Jesus described here has done more than finish a difficult race ahead of us. He is the pioneer of our salvation.

## The Word

**ἀρχηγός** (*archēgos*) means *pioneer*, *founder*, *originator*, or *leader*. Different English renderings bring out different aspects of someone who stands at the beginning or leads the way.

The word is used of Jesus in Acts 3:15 and 5:31 and Hebrews 2:10 and 12:2. The context tells us what his leadership accomplishes. A gloss alone should not be made to carry the whole doctrine of faith.

## Range of Meaning

Acts 3:15 calls Jesus the Originator of life, in the sharp contrast between the people's killing him and God's raising him. Acts 5:31 calls him Leader and Savior, exalted to give repentance and forgiveness.

Hebrews 2:10 speaks of the pioneer of salvation being made perfect through sufferings. This does not imply that Jesus began morally defective. His suffering belongs to the accomplishment of the saving work through which he brings others to glory.

In Hebrews 12:2, *archēgos* is paired with **τελειωτής** (*teleiōtēs*), *perfecter*. Jesus stands at both the beginning and the completion of the faith to which the passage directs us.

## Where You’ll Meet It

The exhortation is to run with endurance while:

> “Let us fix our eyes on Jesus, the author and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy set before Him endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.” (Hebrews 12:2, BSB)

The sentence immediately identifies his endurance of the cross and his place at God's right hand. The object of attention is his saving course through suffering to glory, not our ability to maintain an unbroken religious feeling.

Hebrews 2 develops why that matters. Jesus shares our humanity, undergoes death, and releases those held captive by its fear. The pioneer does not simply point toward safety. He accomplishes the deliverance by which his people reach it.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Faith does not begin as our independent contribution to which Christ adds finishing assistance. The Spirit creates faith through the word of Christ. Romans 10:17 directs us to that preached word, while Acts 5:31 presents the exalted Jesus as the giver of repentance and forgiveness.

Looking to Jesus therefore means attending to the Savior given in his Word, not attempting a feat of inward concentration. The one faith trusts is also the one whose promise calls it into being and sustains it.

The exhortation to endurance remains real. Christians resist sin, hear the Word, pray, and continue in their callings. These activities are the life of faith, not the purchase price of Christ's help.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Jesus can be reduced to a coach who helps us finish what we started. Hebrews gives a more substantial Savior: the one whose suffering opens the way and whose completed work grounds our hope.

His example matters because he first does for us what no imitation could accomplish. Treating him only as an example leaves the burden of salvation where exhausted sinners cannot carry it.

## So What

When faith feels weak, the answer is not to become preoccupied with measuring it. Hear again what Christ has done and promised. His cross and resurrection remain true when your attention falters.

Keep coming to the Word that sets him before you. The pioneer of salvation has not left its completion to your strength.
