Christians live within relationships they did not invent: families, congregations, workplaces, and civil communities. Scripture speaks of being subject within those relationships, while keeping every human authority beneath God's Word.

## The Word

**ὑποτάσσω** (*hypotassō*) means *to subject* or *put in subordination*. Middle and passive forms often express being subject or submitting. The verb identifies an ordered relation, whose character must be understood from the people and responsibilities involved.

The word alone does not mean that every use describes voluntary agreement, and it does not mean that every authority is unlimited. Neither conclusion can be established simply by choosing a preferred English gloss.

## Range of Meaning

Luke 2:51 describes Jesus as subject to Mary and Joseph. Romans 13 concerns governing authorities. Ephesians 5:21 speaks of being subject to one another in reverence for Christ, and 1 Corinthians 15 uses the verb in its account of all things subjected under Christ.

These relations are not identical. Christ's final dominion, a child's place in a household, and a citizen's relation to government must not be collapsed into one model of human command and compliance.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul writes:

> “Everyone must submit himself to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which is from God. The authorities that exist have been appointed by God.” (Romans 13:1, BSB)

Romans 13 connects authority with God's ordering of temporal life and the restraint of wrongdoing. First Peter 2:13–17 similarly speaks of submission, doing good, fearing God, and honoring the emperor.

Acts 5:29 supplies the necessary limit when the apostles are forbidden to bear Christ's witness: God must be obeyed rather than human beings. That response does not deny all authority. It refuses an exercise of authority that contradicts the higher command of God.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Augsburg Confession XVI affirms the goodness of civil order and the duty to obey lawful commands. It also states that a command requiring sin must not be followed. The confession therefore gives neither general permission for lawlessness nor an unlimited religious endorsement of state power.

The same need for careful distinction applies when Scripture addresses households. A passage about ordered relations cannot rightly be used to make coercion, violence, or concealment of wrongdoing into Christian virtues. Protecting a person in danger and seeking appropriate help do not amount to denying God's order.

Those who exercise authority also remain under judgment. They are accountable for the good of the people entrusted to them. Christian teaching about submission should never be spoken as though only the subordinate person has duties before God.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Disagreement is not automatically rebellion, and obedience is not automatically faithfulness. The substance of the command matters. Conversely, personal annoyance does not by itself justify disregarding a legitimate responsibility.

We should also resist turning this verb into a formula that answers every complex case without attention to context. Scripture provides duties and limits; responsible application requires truthful attention to the circumstances.

## So What

Honor the responsibilities God has given you and others. Obey what may be obeyed without sin, speak truthfully about wrongdoing, and seek protection for those being harmed.

[Exousia](/greek/exousia/) considers authority itself, while [Leitourgos](/greek/leitourgos/) reminds us that even public rulers hold an office of service.
