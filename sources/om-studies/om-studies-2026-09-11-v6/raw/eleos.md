The Kyrie gives a congregation a prayer short enough to carry into almost any trouble: *Lord, have mercy*. Its confidence rests in the Lord addressed. Christians ask for mercy because Scripture reveals a God who is rich in it and who has acted on it in Christ.

## The Word

**ἔλεος** (*eleos*) means *mercy*. The related verb **ἐλεέω** (*eleeō*) means *to show mercy*. The familiar liturgical form *eleison* comes from that verb; it is not another form of the noun.

In *Kyrie eleison*, *Kyrie* addresses the Lord and *eleison* asks him to have mercy. [Kyrios](/greek/kyrios/) considers the title. Together the words make a plea, not a statement that the speaker has earned help.

## Range of Meaning

Luke 1 celebrates mercy in the songs surrounding Jesus' birth. Mary speaks of God's mercy toward those who fear him, and Zechariah connects divine mercy with forgiveness, light, and peace.

Romans 15:9 speaks of the Gentiles glorifying God for his mercy. The word reaches beyond pity felt at a distance. God's mercy brings salvation to people who need it.

Titus 3:5 makes the contrast explicit: salvation rests on his mercy rather than righteous works we have performed. Paul joins that mercy to the washing of new birth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Hebrews invites believers:

> “Let us then approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need.” (Hebrews 4:16, BSB)

The invitation follows the confession of Jesus as our great high priest, able to sympathize with our weaknesses and yet without sin. Confidence comes from the one through whom we approach God.

Ephesians 2:4–5 gives another decisive setting. God, rich in mercy, makes people alive with Christ even while they are dead in offenses. His mercy answers a need they could not repair by improving themselves first.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Kyrie teaches us to ask as recipients of God's goodness. It is at home in a service where the Lord's Word is heard and his gifts are received. It can also be prayed when the need is too tangled to describe at length.

Mercy does not mean that God becomes careless about evil. Ephesians names both sin and wrath before announcing life in Christ. Forgiveness is grounded in the Savior's work, not in a decision to pretend that nothing was wrong.

Nor does asking for mercy mean that we must persuade a reluctant God to become compassionate. His revealed action in Christ gives us reason to approach him. The prayer receives its confidence from the gospel.

## So What

When you know your need but cannot organize an explanation, ask the Lord for mercy. He does not require an impressive prayer before hearing his children.

You can also carry your neighbor's need into that petition. Mercy received becomes a reason to act mercifully: to forgive, help, and remain attentive to suffering. The brief prayer opens outward toward the people for whom Christ has taught you to care.
