A gift can be received without becoming a wage. Paul describes the people who reign in life through Christ as recipients of grace and righteousness. Their receiving does not turn God's generosity into a reward for a successful religious performance.

## The Word

**λαμβάνω** (*lambanō*) means *to take* or *to receive*. It is a verb whose force depends on the object and circumstances. Someone may take an object in hand, receive a message, obtain a benefit, or accept a person.

There is no universal rule that the word means passive reception rather than active taking. When Paul speaks about receiving grace, the character of grace and the surrounding contrast explain the relationship. We should not try to prove justification from the verb's dictionary definition alone.

## Range of Meaning

Matthew 26:26 uses the verb when Jesus tells the disciples to take the bread. John 1:12 uses it for receiving Christ and connects that reception with believing in his name. First Corinthians 4:7 asks what the Corinthians have that they did not receive.

The last passage confronts boasting. Gifts may be genuinely possessed and used without becoming grounds for treating oneself as their independent source. Receiving establishes dependence, not insignificance.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul speaks of:

> “those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness” (Romans 5:17, NET)

Romans 5:12–21 contrasts Adam's trespass and its consequences with Christ's obedient work and its gift. The point is not to make faith a second achievement that matches Christ's obedience. It is to identify those who receive what comes through him.

John 1:12–13 supplies another important context. Receiving Christ is connected with believing, while the next verse describes birth from God rather than human willing or ancestry. The vocabulary of reception belongs within God's saving initiative.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Augsburg Confession IV describes forgiveness and righteousness received through faith for Christ's sake. Faith is necessary, but its necessity does not make it meritorious. A beggar's need to receive bread does not make the bread a salary.

Even that comparison has limits. The sinner does not generate saving faith by an independent spiritual ability. Augsburg Confession V identifies the gospel and sacraments as the means through which the Holy Spirit works faith. God's giving reaches the very trust by which his promise is received.

This keeps the believer from looking inward for a sufficiently impressive act of receiving. The question is whom faith trusts and what he promises. Weak faith has the same Christ as strong faith.

## Where People Get It Wrong

An invitation to receive Christ can be turned into a demand to locate one decisive experience that proves salvation. John directs attention to Christ and birth from God, not to the ability to reconstruct the emotional intensity of a particular day.

Reception also must not be confused with automatic saving benefit apart from faith. Rejecting a gift is not the same as receiving it. That distinction does not make the receiver the gift's author.

## So What

Hear the promise Christ gives now. You need not improve its value by making your response impressive.

Then use what you have received for others without boasting as though you had created it. [Dōrean](/greek/dorean/) considers the related language of a gift given freely.
