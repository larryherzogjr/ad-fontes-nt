Weakness can make a Christian wonder whether God has withdrawn. Paul and Hebrews direct the sufferer elsewhere: toward the Spirit's help, Christ's sufficient grace, and the high priest who knows our weakness without sharing our sin.

## The Word

**ἀσθένεια** (*astheneia*) means *weakness* or *infirmity*. Depending on the context, it can concern illness, vulnerability, or a person's inability to accomplish what is needed.

The related adjective **ἀσθενής** (*asthenēs*) means *weak*. First Corinthians 1:25 uses a form of that adjective, rather than the noun, when contrasting what appears weak in God's work with human strength.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 8:26 places weakness within the life of believers who groan while awaiting bodily redemption. They do not know how to pray as they ought, and the Spirit helps them.

Second Corinthians 12 connects weakness with Paul's affliction, insults, hardships, and persecutions for Christ. The passage does not identify his thorn precisely enough for us to diagnose it with confidence.

Hebrews 4:15 speaks of Christ's sympathy with our weaknesses while expressly affirming that he is without sin. Hebrews 5:2 discusses ordinary high priests who are themselves subject to weakness; their need to offer for their own sins distinguishes them from Christ.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul reports the Lord's answer:

> “My grace is enough for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.” (2 Corinthians 12:9, NET)

Paul had asked repeatedly for the affliction to depart. The answer does not say that asking for relief was wrong. It gives him a promise of sufficient grace even though the burden remains.

Romans 8:26 offers a related comfort when prayer itself becomes difficult. The Spirit's help is not restricted to believers who can explain their need fluently. God's care reaches them in the inadequacy they already know.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The theology of the cross teaches us to recognize God's saving work through his Word rather than measure his favor by visible success. Christ's crucifixion appeared to be defeat, yet through it he accomplished salvation.

That does not make suffering good in itself or require Christians to seek it. Weakness is not a spiritual qualification we must acquire before Christ will help us. His promise meets people whose need is already real.

Nor should the confession of his sympathy blur his sinlessness. He truly knows temptation and human vulnerability, but he does not need forgiveness for sins of his own. That is part of why he can be our trustworthy high priest.

## So What

Ask for relief and receive the help available through other people. Faith does not require refusing ordinary care or pretending that pain is pleasant.

When relief is delayed, hear the promise Paul received. Christ's grace is not measured by your strength, and the Spirit's intercession does not depend on your ability to form a complete prayer. Weakness does not place you outside God's care.
