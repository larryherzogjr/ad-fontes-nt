A steward is responsible for goods that belong to someone else. That makes the work both important and limited. Paul uses this household role to describe ministry: Christ's servants administer gifts they neither created nor own.

## The Word

**οἰκονόμος** (*oikonomos*) means *steward* or *household manager*. The steward has a charge to fulfill and an owner to whom he must answer.

The word can describe an ordinary manager handling property. Applied to the church, it asks whose gifts are being administered and whether those entrusted with them are faithful.

## Range of Meaning

Luke 16:1–8 supplies the ordinary household setting in the parable of the dishonest manager. The manager handles his master's accounts and faces dismissal for wasting the estate. His shrewdness in preparing for the future is commended; his dishonesty does not become a rule for Christian conduct.

Titus 1:7 applies the word to an overseer entrusted with God's work. The following verses require self-control, hospitality, and firm adherence to the faithful message. Stewardship involves both the teaching delivered and the life of the person delivering it.

First Peter 4:10 addresses believers more broadly. Each is to use the gift received in service to others as a steward of God's varied grace. The image therefore reaches beyond the pastoral office into Christian service.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul writes:

> “Now what is sought in stewards is that one be found faithful.” (1 Corinthians 4:2, NET)

The preceding verse names the apostles as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries. These mysteries are God's revealed saving gifts, not secret discoveries that a teacher may reserve for an inner circle.

Paul then refuses to make human applause or his own clear conscience the final judgment upon his ministry. The Lord will disclose what is hidden. A steward's accountability runs deeper than either public reputation or private satisfaction.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The pastor's work is to deliver what Christ has entrusted to his church. The gospel may be taught clearly in fresh language, but its substance is not available for revision. The sacraments are to be administered according to Christ's institution, not redesigned as expressions of a minister's ingenuity.

This gives faithfulness a concrete object. It means attending to the actual Word, actual gifts, and actual people committed to one's care. [Poimēn](/greek/poimen/) approaches the same responsibility through the image of a shepherd.

The broader use in 1 Peter also honors service that receives little public recognition. Ability, time, and material resources can be put to work for the neighbor as gifts received from God.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Financial giving belongs within stewardship, but pledge cards cannot contain the word's New Testament range. A church could meet its budget while neglecting the teaching and care entrusted to it.

Nor does faithfulness mean refusing to examine the effects of one's work. A steward should learn, correct mistakes, and attend to real needs. The distinction is that popularity and numerical results cannot replace fidelity to the owner as the governing standard.

## So What

You do not have to invent a grander calling before serving faithfully. Attend to the person and responsibility already before you, using what God has given.

For the church's ministry, this also brings relief. Christ supplies the treasures. His servants are called to deliver them faithfully, and those who receive them can trust the giver whose gifts they remain.
