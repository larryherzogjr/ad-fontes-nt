A key is useful because a locked door can actually be opened. In Christ's promise of the Keys, loosing is not merely a discussion of whether forgiveness might be possible. It belongs to the authority he gives his Church for sinners.

## The Word

**λύω** (*lyō*) means *to loose*, *release*, *undo*, or *dissolve*. It is a verb used in many ordinary settings. Someone can untie an animal, undo a sandal fastening, or release what is bound.

In the paired language of binding and loosing, the context concerns the kingdom and the Church's dealings with sin. We should neither ignore that context nor make every occurrence of the verb a concealed reference to absolution.

## Range of Meaning

John 11:44 uses *lyō* in Jesus' command to release Lazarus from his burial wrappings. Matthew 21:2 uses it for untying the animals the disciples are to bring. John 2:19 uses it in the saying about destroying the temple.

Such examples show the breadth of the verb. They do not establish one physical picture that must govern every figurative use. In Matthew 16 and 18, its pairing with binding and its place within Christ's commission guide the reading.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus promises:

> “whatever you release on earth will have been released in heaven.” (Matthew 16:19, NET)

Matthew 16 places the saying after Peter's confession of Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God. Matthew 18 gives the binding and loosing language to the disciples in the context of the Church addressing sin.

Read alongside John 20:23, these passages give a definite commission concerning forgiveness and retention. They do not depict a human authority inventing terms of salvation that Christ has left unspecified.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The Smalcald Articles III.VII confess that Christ gives the Church authority to bind and loose sins. The following article emphasizes absolution as comfort and help for troubled consciences. The gift is meant to be used, particularly where guilt is heavy and faith is weak.

This comfort includes sins we have not managed to identify exhaustively. The confession rejects the idea that a perfect inventory is the necessary foundation for forgiveness. God knows the full extent of our sin; Christ's promise is not limited by the accuracy of our memory.

The pastor who absolves serves that promise. His task is not to announce that sin was insignificant, but to give Christ's forgiveness for what really was sin. That distinction lets confession remain honest without leaving the penitent trapped in accusation.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Forgiveness can be made to sound provisional, as though Christ releases the debt only after the sinner has proved future success. Amendment of life should follow repentance, but it does not retroactively purchase absolution.

Loosing also is not indifference to harm. Civil consequences, restitution, and protection of neighbors may still be necessary. Forgiving sin does not make every earthly responsibility disappear.

## So What

When Christ's absolution is spoken to you, receive its release. Do not turn the promise into another assignment to prove that you feel forgiven enough.
