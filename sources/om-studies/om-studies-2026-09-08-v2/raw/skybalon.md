Paul does not approach Philippians 3 with an empty résumé. He names ancestry, circumcision, religious seriousness, and a reputation for observance. Then he refuses to let that record establish his righteousness before God. Knowing Christ has changed what he counts as gain.

## The Word

**σκύβαλον** (*skybalon*) means *refuse* or *dung*. Its New Testament occurrence is the plural **σκύβαλα** (*skybala*) in Philippians 3:8. The word describes what is discarded as worthless or repulsive.

The NET renders it *dung*. That sharpness matters, but it does not justify treating every translation using *rubbish* as dishonest. Refuse belongs to the word's range too. Paul's argument supplies the force that no isolated English gloss can fully convey.

## Range of Meaning

A strong word for waste is not automatically a statement that everything named earlier is evil in itself. Paul is weighing what could serve as gain in relation to Christ. His ancestry and circumcision cannot supply the righteousness he needs.

The list also includes his persecution of the church. That is not a morally innocent credential, even though he once regarded it as evidence of zeal. We should not describe the whole list as good deeds or make his former confidence the measure of what was actually good.

## Where You’ll Meet It

After placing everything beside the surpassing value of knowing Christ, Paul says:

> “indeed, I regard them as dung!” (Philippians 3:8, NET)

The next words state the purpose: gaining Christ and being found in him. Verse 9 contrasts Paul's own righteousness derived from the law with righteousness from God.

This is more searching than admitting a few failures while keeping one's best accomplishments in reserve. The things that appeared to establish a superior claim cannot furnish righteousness before God. [Kauchaomai](/greek/kauchaomai/) explores the related question of where confidence rests.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The sinner's need is not answered by balancing failures with achievements. Christ is not added to the remaining merits until the total becomes acceptable. Faith receives a righteousness whose source is God and whose ground is Christ.

That gift frees us to distinguish the value of an action for our neighbor from its inability to justify us. Honest work, study, generosity, and obedience matter. They cease to be gifts rightly used when we offer them as a rival foundation for standing before God.

Nor does Paul's contrast authorize contempt for Jewish people or the denial of God's gifts to Israel. The subject is confidence in credentials as grounds of righteousness. His ancestry cannot save him, but it does not follow that ancestry itself is pollution.

## Where People Get It Wrong

The passage is sometimes reduced to leaving behind an obviously disgraceful past. Paul includes the very credentials that once made him confident. His claim reaches what he once regarded as his best, without making the persecution also listed innocent.

The opposite mistake is to hear Paul condemning all good works. He is denying their power to supply the righteousness found in Christ. Their place in love and service remains real.

## So What

You do not need to defend your best accomplishments as though your standing before God would collapse without them. Receive Christ's righteousness, and let your gifts serve the people who need them.

What cannot justify you may still benefit your neighbor. Freed from the demand to make it save, you can put it to work in love.
