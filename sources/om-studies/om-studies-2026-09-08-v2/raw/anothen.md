Nicodemus hears a statement about birth and asks how an old person could enter the womb again. Jesus directs him to the Spirit's work. The conversation turns on a word that can point both to another beginning and to an origin above us.

## The Word

**ἄνωθεν** (*anōthen*) is an adverb meaning *from above*, *again*, or *from the beginning*, depending on context. In John 3, the birth language allows an interplay between being born again and being born from above.

The NET chooses *from above* in John 3:3. That rendering draws attention to the source of the new birth. Nicodemus's question in the next verse shows why the sense of another birth is also part of the conversation.

## Range of Meaning

John 3:31 uses the word for the one who comes from above. John 19:23 uses it of a garment woven from the top. Luke 1:3 uses it in connection with tracing matters from the beginning, and James 1:17 places good gifts as coming from above.

These uses guard against treating *again* as the word's sole meaning. They also keep us from assuming every occurrence names regeneration. A word's range creates possibilities; the sentence determines which are being used.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Jesus says:

> “unless a person is born from above, he cannot see the kingdom of God.” (John 3:3, NET)

John 3:5 then speaks of birth from water and Spirit, followed by a contrast between what is born of flesh and what is born of Spirit. Nicodemus cannot solve the problem by arranging a second natural birth. A new source of life is needed.

The passage later directs attention to the Son of Man lifted up and to life through believing in him. The conversation is not an invitation to discover an inward experience detached from Christ's saving work.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

New birth is God's work. The Small Catechism's Third Article explanation excludes our own understanding or strength as the source of faith in Christ. The Spirit calls through the gospel and keeps believers in the faith.

Lutherans also hear the water-and-Spirit language in connection with Baptism. The catechism describes Baptism as water joined to God's Word and, in explaining its power, draws on Titus 3's washing of new birth. It does not make ordinary water a second savior alongside Christ.

The lexical meaning of *anōthen* does not, by itself, prove the sacramental reading of John 3:5. That reading concerns the whole passage and its place in Scripture. We should state the theological connection without pretending that one adverb has already answered every exegetical question.

## Where People Get It Wrong

Being born again can become a demand to identify one dramatic feeling or conversion story. John directs us to the source and gift of life, not to a standard emotional sequence every Christian must reproduce.

It is also a mistake to separate the Spirit from the outward Word as though spiritual birth required escape from anything spoken, heard, or administered.

## So What

You cannot arrange your own spiritual birth by returning to the beginning and trying harder. Hear Christ's promise and receive the gifts through which his Spirit works.

[Anagennaō](/greek/anagennao/) follows Peter's language of being born anew through the enduring Word.
