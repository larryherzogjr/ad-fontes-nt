A shadow tells you that something is there. It has a real relation to what casts it, but it cannot do everything the thing itself can do. You cannot take shelter under the shadow of a house while ignoring the house.

The New Testament uses that difference to explain the place of the old covenant's ceremonies. Their meaning is found in the Christ toward whom they pointed.

## The Word

**σκιά** (*skia*) means *shadow*. In the passages considered here, it describes a preliminary form whose fulfillment lies beyond itself. Calling something a shadow does not mean it was a lie or a mistake.

Colossians 2:17 sets *skia* alongside *sōma*, the word ordinarily meaning body. The NET renders the contrast as shadow and reality. The thing that gives the shadow its significance belongs to Christ.

## Range of Meaning

The shadow image can emphasize dependence, outline, or anticipation. We should let each passage tell us which aspect matters rather than turning the mechanics of sunlight into a complete theology.

Hebrews 8:5 describes the earthly sanctuary as a sketch and shadow of the heavenly. Hebrews 10:1 calls the law a shadow of the good things to come. In that chapter the concern is the repeated sacrifices and their inability to bring worshipers to the completion Christ gives.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul tells the Colossians not to let anyone judge them over food, drink, festivals, new moons, or Sabbaths. He explains:

> “these are only the shadow of the things to come, but the reality is Christ!” (Colossians 2:17, NET)

The practices are being used to pass judgment on Christians whose life is in Christ. Paul answers by directing them to the fulfillment, not by making the shadow a second requirement alongside him.

Hebrews 10:1–10 develops the sacrificial side of the same movement. Repeated offerings recall sin; the blood of animals cannot take it away. Christ's obedient offering of his own body accomplishes what those sacrifices could not achieve by their repetition.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The old covenant's ceremonies were God's appointments in their time. Their fulfillment in Christ is a reason to honor their purpose, not to sneer at them. They taught Israel to await what God would give.

They also cannot be imposed as conditions for receiving Christ's completed gift. If even a divinely appointed shadow yields to its fulfillment, humanly devised observances cannot be made necessary for justification. Church practices can serve good order without becoming additional terms on which God receives sinners.

That is not a dismissal of God's moral commands. Paul's particular argument concerns the observances named in the passage. Nor does it dissolve what Christ himself instituted for his church. The Lord who fulfills the old covenant still gives his Word, Baptism, and Supper.

## So What

Read the ceremonies with interest. Ask what they taught, what they could not accomplish, and how Scripture brings them to Christ. [Typos](/greek/typos/) and [Plēroō](/greek/pleroo/) trace that movement from other angles.

Then let Christ's completed work settle the conscience. You do not need to return to the shadow to supplement the reality he has given.
