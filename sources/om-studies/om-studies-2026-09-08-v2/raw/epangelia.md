A promise gives the hearer someone to trust. Its strength lies in the speaker's faithfulness and ability to do what he says. When Paul speaks of God's promise to Abraham, he directs faith there, away from the attempt to earn an inheritance through obedience to the law.

## The Word

**ἐπαγγελία** (*epangelia*) means *promise*. In the passages about Abraham, it names what God pledged to give and the reliable word by which he pledged it.

The related verb *epangellomai* means *to promise*. Neither word makes the hearer the author of what is promised. God speaks, and faith receives his word as trustworthy.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 4 contrasts inheritance through law with inheritance through promise. Galatians 3 insists that the later giving of the law did not cancel God's prior promise. The promise reaches its fulfillment in Christ and includes the promised Spirit.

Ephesians 1:13 describes the Holy Spirit as promised. Hebrews 6:13–18 emphasizes God's unchanging purpose and his inability to lie. These passages do not teach a method for making any desired outcome certain. They direct us to what God has actually said.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Paul explains:

> “For this reason it is by faith so that it may be by grace, with the result that the promise may be certain to all the descendants” (Romans 4:16, NET)

Faith and grace belong together because faith receives what God gives. Making the inheritance depend on fulfilling the law would undermine the certainty Paul is describing.

Abraham trusts the God who can do what he promises, despite the apparent impossibility of the circumstances (4:19–21). Galatians 3:26–29 identifies those who belong to Christ as heirs according to the promise and joins that belonging with Baptism into Christ.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The promise gives faith its object. We do not turn inward to find an act of believing impressive enough to make God gracious. We listen to the word in which he gives Christ and his benefits.

This is also why the sacraments matter. God's promise comes through the means Christ instituted, addressed to actual people. Baptism and the Supper are not objects to which we attach our own hopeful meanings. God's word identifies his gift and gives us reason to receive it confidently.

[Logizomai](/greek/logizomai/) follows Paul's account of righteousness credited to faith. The promise supplies the certainty that a running total of our achievements cannot supply.

## Where People Get It Wrong

To claim a promise faithfully is to trust what God has said. It is not a technique for obligating him to provide wealth, health, or a preferred timetable he has not promised.

Nor is God's saving promise a bargain whose price is our prior worthiness. Faith receives the gift; it does not earn it. This does not mean everyone receives salvation apart from faith, or that God's word cannot be resisted.

Christians may rightly speak of the gospel as offering forgiveness. The danger is treating that offer as a conditional transaction, as though forgiveness were earned by fulfilling our part of a bargain. What matters is whether the hearer is directed to Christ's gift or to a qualification he must supply.

## So What

Ask what God has promised and where he gives it. Then hear that word as addressed to you.

Your confidence need not rise and fall with the strength of your resolve. The one who promises is faithful, and Christ is the gift on which faith rests.
