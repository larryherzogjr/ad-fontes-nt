The first fruit brought in from a field belongs to the harvest that is beginning. It is not a painting of the crop or a hopeful substitute for it. It has grown from that field, and more is to follow.

Paul uses that relation between beginning and completion to speak about Easter. Christ's resurrection is the beginning of something that includes his people.

## The Word

**ἀπαρχή** (*aparchē*) means *firstfruits*. It can name the first portion of produce set apart as an offering, or the first part of a larger gathering. The image joins priority with belonging: this comes first, and it belongs to the whole that follows.

The offering background matters. Firstfruits acknowledge God's gift and claim upon the harvest. In the New Testament, the image can describe Christ, the Spirit's present gift, or people whom God has gathered to himself.

## Range of Meaning

Romans 11:16 uses the first portion of dough in an argument about the whole batch. James 1:18 describes those brought to birth through the word of truth as a kind of firstfruits of God's creatures. Revelation 14:4 applies the word to people redeemed for God and the Lamb.

The image does not have a single referent everywhere. Each passage tells us what is first, what larger whole is in view, and how the two belong together.

## Where You’ll Meet It

> “But now Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.” (1 Corinthians 15:20, NET)

Verse 23 explains the order: Christ first, then those who belong to him when he comes. Paul is not merely saying that Jesus provides an encouraging example. The risen Christ stands at the beginning of the resurrection harvest of his people.

Romans 8:23 uses firstfruits in speaking of the Spirit. Christians already have his gift, yet they still groan while awaiting the redemption of their bodies. Possession and waiting belong in the same sentence.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

Easter gives the church a future anchored in an accomplished event. The body laid in Joseph's tomb was raised. Those who belong to Christ await bodily life with him, not simply the survival of a memory or an influence.

The harvest image receives its certainty from the risen Lord and his promise. It is not an argument that every promising beginning in nature must succeed. Christ himself has defeated death, and Paul identifies his people with the harvest that follows him.

[Arrabōn](/greek/arrabon/) supplies the companion image of a down payment. Both words help us hold together what God has already given and what we still await.

## So What

At a Christian funeral, the church speaks about resurrection because Christ has risen. We have not been left to invent a consoling picture of what might happen.

Grief remains real. Romans 8 makes room for the groaning of those who have the Spirit. But the firstfruits are already gathered. The Lord who rose will not forget those who belong to him when he comes to gather the harvest.
