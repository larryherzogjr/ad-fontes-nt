The exhortation to keep gathering in Hebrews follows a promise: through Jesus, sinners have access to God. Christians come together as people who have received that access and who need to encourage one another in the hope they confess.

## The Word

**ἐπισυναγωγή** (*episynagōgē*) means *gathering* or *assembling together*. The noun occurs in Hebrews 10:25 and 2 Thessalonians 2:1.

One passage concerns the Christian assembly in the present; the other concerns our gathering to Christ at his coming. The same word can describe both without making the two events identical.

## Range of Meaning

In Hebrews, the context is mutual encouragement, love, and good works. The writer warns against abandoning the community's meetings. Gathering supplies a setting in which Christians strengthen one another in the confession of their hope.

In 2 Thessalonians, Paul speaks of the Lord's arrival and our being gathered to him. He urges the church not to be shaken by a claim that the day of the Lord has already come.

These uses point faith toward life with Christ and his people. The present assembly lives in expectation of the gathering he will bring to completion.

## Where You’ll Meet It

Hebrews urges Christians:

> “not abandoning our own meetings, as some are in the habit of doing” (Hebrews 10:25, NET)

The sentence continues with the call to encourage one another as the day draws near. This is a real exhortation, not a suggestion that gathering becomes unnecessary whenever someone feels spiritually self-sufficient.

The preceding verses explain why Christians can draw near: Jesus has opened the way by his blood, and he is the great priest over God's house. The promise comes first. The call to persevere follows from the trustworthy one who made it.

## What Confessional Lutherans Hear

The congregation gathers to receive Christ's Word and gifts and to answer with prayer and praise. It is also a place where believers bear one another's burdens, speak encouragement, and learn to serve in love.

Hebrews does not provide an inventory of every element in a Sunday service. Its immediate argument joins access through Christ, a steadfast confession, and mutual encouragement. The church's wider life of preaching and sacraments belongs within that dependence on his gifts.

This makes attendance more than an item to record. It also makes the absence of a struggling neighbor a matter for care. Someone confined by illness needs the congregation's ministry to reach him; he should not be treated as though inability to attend were abandonment of Christ.

## So What

Come expecting to receive what you cannot supply for yourself. Come also prepared to encourage people who need the same promise you do.

If gathering has become difficult, let your pastor or another Christian know what is keeping you away. The answer to isolation may begin with someone bringing you the Word, listening to your burden, and helping you return. The church gathers around a Savior who gives access, sustains hope, and will gather his people to himself.
